## 1. Task 1: the rank lemmas are known theorems

### 1.1 Statements [K]
Notation: |ψ⟩ ∈ A⊗B⊗C⊗D a four-party tensor (equivalently a mixed ρ_ABC purified by D); r_X = rank of the flattening X | rest.
- **CHLW Theorem 1** (Cadney, Huber, Linden, Winter, "Inequalities for the ranks of multipartite quantum states",
  Linear Algebra Appl. 452 (2014) 153–171, arXiv:1308.0539, §III): **r_A ≤ r_AB · r_AC.** This is x7's L1 exactly.
  The paper states it for complex four-party states. It gives two proofs: one via strong subadditivity after filtering
  ψ_A to be maximally mixed (ℂ only), and a "direct proof" (Lemma 3 plus eqs. (26)–(31)).
- **CHLW Theorem 2:** r_A² ≤ r_AB · r_AC · r_BC. Proved only via SSA + weak monotonicity, so over ℂ (and ℝ).
- **CHLW Hypothesis 1 = x7's L2:** **r_BC ≤ r_AB · r_AC**, i.e. rk(AD|BC) ≤ rk(AB|CD)·rk(AC|BD). Open in CHLW; they
  proved the cases r_AB ≤ 2 (Thm 5), and a later paper proved r_AB = 3. CHLW Lemma 4 shows it is equivalent to
  **rank(M^Γ) ≤ Sr(M) · rank(M)** for M ∈ M_{m1,n1} ⊗ M_{m2,n2}, where Sr is the operator-Schmidt rank and Γ the
  partial transpose.
- **Proved by Song, Chen, Sun, Hu**, "A complete picture of the four-party linear inequalities in terms of the
  0-entropy", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 69(4) (2023) 2385–2399, arXiv:2105.07679, Theorem 9. The proof is by induction
  on Sr(M), using a canonical form of block matrices under local equivalence (Thm 8). It is written over ℂ.
  (The repo already cites it once: open-door §4 table, "[8, Thm 9] as read by Majdoddin".)
- **Complete list for four parties** (CHLW §IV with Song et al.). Up to permutations of the parties and substituting
  disjoint unions of parties:
    S0(A) ≥ 0;  S0(A)+S0(B) ≥ S0(AB);  S0(AB)+S0(AC) ≥ S0(A);  S0(AB)+S0(AC)+S0(BC) ≥ 2S0(A);  S0(AB)+S0(AC) ≥ S0(BC).
  These cut out a cone whose extreme rays fall into 6 families. All six are attained asymptotically by explicit states
  (CHLW eqs. (33)–(38)), so this is the closed cone generated by four-party log-rank vectors. CHLW's rays 7 and 8 are
  cut off by the last inequality.

### 1.2 Field dependence
- **L1 (CHLW Thm 1) holds over every field [R].** The direct proof uses only linear independence:
  1. Write T = Σ_{k≤r} η_k ⊗ θ_k with r = r_AB and the θ_k ∈ C⊗D linearly independent. View each θ_k as a matrix S_k.
  2. CHLW Lemma 3 (field-free induction) gives a set K of ≤ r coordinates of C on which the column-truncations of the
     S_k stay independent.
  3. So there is a linear map V on C⊗D with θ_k = V(P_K θ_k), and T = V_{CD}(P_K T). Hence r_A(T) ≤ r_A(P_K T).
  4. Any tensor X satisfies r_A(X) ≤ r_AC(X)·dim C. Coordinate projection does not raise r_AC.
  5. Therefore r_A ≤ |K|·r_AC ≤ r_AB·r_AC. ∎
- **L2 (Song et al. Thm 9): proved over ℂ only.** Their canonical-form argument rescales blocks by constants (Lemma 6)
  and I have not audited it for finite fields. [N] `code/field_check.py`: 180,913 instances over GF(2) and GF(3) tested
  rank(M^Γ) ≤ Sr(M)·rank(M). These were exhaustive rank-one M for 2×2⊗2×2 and 2×3⊗2×3, random for 3×3⊗3×3, plus
  random sparse low-rank and low-Schmidt-rank M. **No violation.** So L2 is [K] over ℂ (hence ℝ, ℚ) and [N/C] over
  finite fields.
- **CHLW Thm 2** has no algebraic proof (CHLW say so) and is [K] over ℂ only.

### 1.3 Consequences on K₄ (three pairings as merges) [R]
- K₄'s merges have 2 groups, so c_k = log rk across pairing k. **Projected onto the three pairing coordinates
  (S0(AB), S0(AC), S0(AD)), the complete cone is exactly the triangle cone c_k ≤ c_k' + c_k''.** Check: the 6 ray
  families project to (0,1,1), (2,2,2), (1,1,0), (2,1,1), (2,1,1), (2,2,2). So L2 is the only constraint on piece
  cost vectors.
- **Game value.** The engine's accounting is V = min_{q∈Δ_3} max_i ⟨q, c(A_i)⟩ (open-door §1.7, Def. 7 form, von
  Neumann). **General q is the real accounting.** Flattening forces, for each k, a piece with c_k ≥ 4λ − o(λ)
  (4 wires cross each pairing).
- **Rank LP** (`code/rank_lp.py k4`, output `rank_lp_k4.out`): minimise over admissible log-rank vectors. Results:
  - **general q: V ≥ 2λ, ρ ≥ 1/3** (= z1's line price), attained at q = (0, ½, ½) by the admissible vectors
    (4,4,0), (4,0,4), (0,4,4). So the rank inequalities add nothing to the unconditional floor.
  - **uniform q** (a sub-class statement: S₃-symmetric strategies, e.g. symmetric families): V ≥ 8/3 λ,
    **ρ ≥ 4/9 ≈ 0.444**, attained by the vectors (4,2,2)-type / (4,4,0)-type.
  - The vectors (4,4,0) etc. are only "not excluded": a piece with cost (4,4,0) is a product across the third
    pairing (rank 1). A family made only of such pieces is excluded exactly by z1 §2 ("t = 0 impossible"). Whether
    near-(4,4,ε) families exist is the same open question as z1's three-rank object.
  - **No known rank inequality pushes K₄ above √2/3 ≈ 0.471, for either q.**
- **Sphere law on K₄ [R].** If every piece satisfies Σ_k c_k² ≤ R², then the forced piece (c_k ≥ 4, c_k'+c_k'' ≥ 4) needs
  R² ≥ 16 + 8 = 24λ². The balanced point of that sphere is R/√3 = √8λ = 2√2λ per merge, **exactly the flat floor
  √2/3.** So a spherical (A₂-graded) family on K₄ can reach the flat floor at best, and L2 does not stop it from doing so.
