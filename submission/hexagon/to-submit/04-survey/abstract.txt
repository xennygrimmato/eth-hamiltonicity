Many lower bounds in exact, parameterised and fine-grained complexity are proved from the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) or the Strong ETH. In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT that refute both. However, a refuted hypothesis does not settle the theorems proved from it: what falls depends on the premise that each proof actually uses, and outside learning theory we know of no systematic account. We present a map of $34$ hypotheses and $27$ published conditional theorems, assuming the claimed algorithms are correct. Each row is refuted, has lost its premise, or survives; a fourth label marks the few theorems whose conclusion is now contradicted by an explicit algorithm. The map rests on one distinction. The new algorithms handle $k$-CNF formulas of bounded width and, through the width reduction of Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi, CNFs of any width with at most $n\cdot 2^{o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})}$ clauses (our derivation from the explicit rate of the Anthropic manuscript). Nothing is known for CNF-SAT with polynomially many clauses of unbounded width. Many of the best-known lower bounds (treewidth optimality, edit distance, Fréchet distance, LCS, diameter approximation) are proved from that "CNF-SETH" and survive with their premise intact. Gap-ETH and the ETH-based proof of the Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis lose their force; Almost-ETH, OV and the Set Cover Conjecture are untouched. We list the few new algorithms that follow and place the first follow-ups: crossover points, barriers, and the Satisfiability Hypothesis (SH). A refutation removes premises; it does not by itself give algorithms, and nothing here is practical. Everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, still under review. Supporting notes are in our public repository, https://github.com/xennygrimmato/eth-hamiltonicity.
