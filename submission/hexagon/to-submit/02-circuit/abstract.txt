By a theorem of Williams, if the Exponential Time Hypothesis fails, then $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ does not have circuits of linear size. In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT. OpenAI's revised preprint (25 October 2026) states the quantitative consequence as its Corollary 1.4, proved on paper: some $L\in\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ has no circuit with at most $c\,n\sqrt{\log n}$ fan-in-two gates for infinitely many $n$. However, this bound is not machine-checked, and Anthropic's Lean development proves only the linear-size form. We machine-check OpenAI's superlinear circuit lower bound in Lean 4 on top of Anthropic's development, whose formalised engine supplies the rate: enp_not_size_n_sqrt_log gives a language $L_0\in\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ and $\varepsilon>0$ (in the proof, $\varepsilon=1/(4608(qG+1))$ for existential constants $q,G$) such that for infinitely many $n$, $L_0$ has no circuit with at most $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}$ gates over the full binary basis, with the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound only. On paper, assuming the clause-count bound $2^{C\cdot kM/\sqrt{\log M}}$ of Anthropic's Main Theorem as a Turing machine with engine constant $C$, we track the constants of Williams' argument: a check circuit of $3s+O(n)$ gates, at most $4S+1$ clauses after Tseitin's transformation, and any $\varepsilon<1/(36aC)$, with $a$ the input-length shift. From the weaker bound in OpenAI's Lean development, rather than the stronger one in its paper, the same argument gives $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}/(\log\log n)^2$. We explain why these algorithms give nothing at $n\log n$, nothing for NEXP or NQP, and nothing more from counting. The constant $\varepsilon$ has no numerical value, the bound holds only infinitely often, and everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
