In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic each claimed a deterministic subexponential algorithm for counting the solutions of a $k$-CNF; both counting engines sum products of local tables. For SAT itself, Majdoddin bounds the rate of Anthropic's grid recursions with product terms, and Dias and Lima bound OpenAI's clause-product scheme. However, Majdoddin notes that no barrier is known for decompositions whose terms are not products, and neither paper treats the counting problems beyond SAT. We introduce the engine barrier problem: bound from below the shared bits $U$ of every network of a stated class that computes a given count, and the rate of every recursion of a stated class. Our approach puts a rank on every cut of a local network, and on one contraction inside both flattenings of a term. Machine-checked in Lean are: correlated (non-product) decompositions of two rectangles, of the $3\times2$ grid and of the full $3\times3$ grid level have flattening-rank sums at least $N^2$, $N^3$ and $N^8$, while $N^9$ is open; the row cut of the permanent has rank $\binom nk$, which on paper gives $U\ge n\log_2(n/s)$ for row-local networks; algebras in which the permanent is a functional of a product have dimension at least $\binom n{n/2}$; pieces have saving $\rho\ge1/2$ (rank form; Anthropic's observation); and exact colouring filters for Hamiltonicity need $q\ge n-4$ colours. On paper we prove $U\ge(1-o(1))n\log_2n$ for vertex-local networks for the Tutte polynomial off the integer hyperbolas, and $\rho\ge3/8$ for correlated decompositions of full grid levels. We cite $45$ Lean theorems, with standard axioms only. Every barrier is about a named model: in Anthropic's general model no unconditional bound on $U$ exists without separating $\mathrm P$ from $\mathrm P^{\#\mathrm P}$, and an unconditional cap on oblivious algebraic algorithms would separate $\mathrm{VP}$ from $\mathrm{VNP}$.
