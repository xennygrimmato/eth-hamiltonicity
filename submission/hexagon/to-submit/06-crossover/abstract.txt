In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic algorithms that count the satisfying assignments of a $k$-CNF in time $2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ or similar, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis. However, neither manuscript computes from which $n$ on its proven bound is smaller than brute force. We compute this crossover $n^*$ for the bounds as they are proved, and report $t^*=\log_2 n^*$. We keep every explicit term and constant, treat existential constants as parameters, and evaluate each bound exactly in the logarithmic domain. For Anthropic's official Lean main theorem on $3$-CNFs with $m=4.26n$ clauses, $t^*=552\,040$ if the unknown polynomial degree $q$ of its Turing-machine time bound is $1$, and $t^*$ grows roughly like $q^2$; re-optimising the free parameters of the same lemmas gives $245\,009$. For OpenAI's interpolation algorithm, $t^*\approx3.02\cdot10^{13}$ as written and $\approx8.53\cdot10^{7}$ with re-optimised parameters, mostly by using an exact binomial tail in place of a Hoeffding window. Our exact algorithm for counting Hamiltonian cycles, which rests on Anthropic's manuscript, beats Held-Karp on cubic graphs from $t^*=55\,440$. Anthropic's $3$-SAT decision machine searches exhaustively for every $n<2^{2^{1722}}$. In every Anthropic-based case $n^*$ is a staircase, fixed by the first recursion level at which a single clique exponent falls below $n$. These numbers are computations about proven upper bounds, not theorems and not running times; none is machine-checked. They are conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review, and nothing practical follows. The script that computes them is in our public repository.
