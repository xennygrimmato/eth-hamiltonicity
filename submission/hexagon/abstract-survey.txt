In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) and with it the Strong ETH. This note records, row by row, which fine-grained hypotheses and which published conditional lower bounds are affected, assuming the claimed algorithms are correct. We classify $33$ hypotheses and $27$ conditional theorems as refuted, as having lost their premise, or as surviving; a fourth label marks the few theorems whose conclusion is now contradicted by an explicit algorithm. The map is organised by one distinction: the new algorithms handle $k$-CNF formulas of bounded width, and, through the width reduction of Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi, CNFs of any width with at most $n\cdot 2^{o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})}$ clauses (a short derivation of ours from the explicit rate of the Anthropic manuscript), but nothing is known for CNF-SAT with polynomially many clauses of unbounded width. Many of the best-known lower bounds (treewidth optimality, edit distance, Fr\'echet distance, LCS, diameter approximation) are proved from that "CNF-SETH" and survive with their premise intact. Gap-ETH and the ETH-based proof of the Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis lose their force; Almost-ETH, OV, 3SUM, APSP and the Set Cover Conjecture are untouched. We list the few genuinely new algorithms that follow. A refutation removes premises; it does not by itself give algorithms, and nothing here is practical. Everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
