In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis. Anthropic's manuscript notes that, by a theorem of Williams, this implies that $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ does not have circuits of linear size. We record the quantitative form. Assuming the clause-count bound $2^{C\cdot kM/\sqrt{\log M}}$ of Anthropic's Main Theorem, in the form of a Turing machine with engine constant $C$, there are $\varepsilon>0$ and a language $L_0\in\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ such that for infinitely many input lengths $n$, $L_0$ has no circuit of size $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}$ over the full binary basis. The bound proved in OpenAI's Lean development gives $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}/(\log\log n)^2$ in the same way. The proof is Williams' argument with the constants tracked: guess a witness circuit, check it with a circuit of $3s+O(n)$ gates, apply Tseitin's transformation to get at most $4S+1$ clauses, and decide satisfiability with the new algorithm; any $\varepsilon<1/(36aC)$ works, where $a$ is the input-length shift of the construction. We explain why these algorithms give nothing at $n\log n$, nothing for $\mathrm{NEXP}$ or $\mathrm{NQP}$, and nothing more from counting. The constant $\varepsilon$ is not explicit, the bound holds only infinitely often, and everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
