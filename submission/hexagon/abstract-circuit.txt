Williams showed that faster satisfiability algorithms imply circuit lower bounds; in particular, if the Exponential Time Hypothesis fails, then $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ does not have circuits of linear size. In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT, and Anthropic's manuscript draws this consequence. However, its argument uses only the qualitative failure of the hypothesis, so it gives "not $O(n)$" with no rate. We record the quantitative form. Assuming the clause-count bound $2^{C\cdot kM/\sqrt{\log M}}$ of Anthropic's Main Theorem, in the form of a Turing machine with engine constant $C$, we prove that there are $\varepsilon>0$ and a language $L_0\in\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ such that for infinitely many input lengths $n$, $L_0$ has no circuit of size $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}$ over the full binary basis. The proof is Williams' argument with the constants tracked: guess a witness circuit, check it with a circuit of $3s+O(n)$ gates, apply Tseitin's transformation to get at most $4S+1$ clauses, and decide satisfiability with the new algorithm; any $\varepsilon<1/(36aC)$ works, where $a$ is the input-length shift of the construction. The existence statement is machine-checked in Lean 4 on top of Anthropic's Lean development, whose formalised engine supplies the rate, as enp_not_size_n_sqrt_log, with the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound only. The bound proved in OpenAI's Lean development gives $\varepsilon\,n\sqrt{\log n}/(\log\log n)^2$ in the same way; this corollary is proved on paper only. We explain why these algorithms give nothing at $n\log n$, nothing for NEXP or NQP, and nothing more from counting. The constant $\varepsilon$ is not explicit, the bound holds only infinitely often, and everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
