Basic problems of computational number theory have proven deterministic bounds that are fixed powers of the input. The class number $h(D)$ of an imaginary quadratic discriminant takes $|D|^{1/2+o(1)}$ steps unconditionally and $|D|^{1/5+o(1)}$ under the generalised Riemann hypothesis (GRH); the least quadratic non-residue modulo a prime $p$ takes $p^{1/(4\sqrt e)+o(1)}$ by Burgess's 1957 bound, polynomial only under GRH; exact divisor sums $\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)$ take $x^{1/3+o(1)}$. However, none of these bounds is of the form $X^{o(1)}$, even conditionally. We present deterministic algorithms in time $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$, where $n$ is the input bit length, for six such problems: the number of reduced forms of discriminant $-N$, for fundamental discriminants $h(-N)$; the least square root of $a$ modulo any $m$; the least quadratic non-residue modulo a prime; and $\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)$, $\sum_{m\le x}\sigma(m)$ and the Gauss circle count. Our approach writes each answer as a count of solutions of equations with one or two products and evaluates it with Anthropic's counting engine through one network library: products by a two-dimensional Schönhage-Strassen network, sums by signed carry chains, inequalities by comparison chains, with $O(n)$ shared bits. We prove the seven resulting theorems in Lean 4 (for example hForm_TM_rate; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound): a finite-alphabet Mathlib Turing machine outputs the answer within $\lfloor C\cdot2^{Cn/\sqrt{\log_2(n+2)}}\rfloor$ steps on every input. Lean proves the count of Gauss's reduced forms; its equality with the ideal class number of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N})$ is classical and not formalised. The constants are astronomical, the machines use exponential space, and every result rests on Anthropic's Lean development of its claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, which is under review.
