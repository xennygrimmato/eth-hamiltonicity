Let $c_{ij}=a_{i1}b_{1j}\oplus a_{i2}b_{2j}$ $(i,j\in\{1,2\})$ be the four entries of the product of two $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbb{F}_2$, viewed as Boolean functions of the eight entries. We show that every circuit over the basis $\{\wedge,\oplus,1\}$ that computes all four of them has at least seven AND gates. The circuit model is unrestricted: an AND gate may multiply any two affine combinations of the inputs and of earlier AND outputs, and intermediate functions may have any degree, with $x^2=x$. Strassen's algorithm gives seven AND gates, so the multiplicative complexity is exactly $7$. The classical lower bounds of Winograd and of Hopcroft and Kerr concern algorithms over polynomials and do not transfer to this model. The lower bound has two supports. First, short lemmas, proved here, reduce the question to a finite search, which two separately written programs carry out: an exact search over spaces of truth tables, and a SAT encoding split into 1080 cubes, each refuted by CaDiCaL 2.1.3 with an LRAT proof that lrat-check accepts. The two programs share no code but rely on the same lemmas. Second, the statement is proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib. The Lean proof proves the reductions and the meaning of its own cube encoding, and it discharges $360$ cubes with bv_decide. Its trusted base is the Lean kernel together with the Lean compiler and runtime, which carry out $365$ Boolean evaluations. By the same external pipeline we also show that the product of a $1\times2$ and a $2\times3$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has multiplicative complexity $6$; this second result is not formalized.
