Before 2026, every proven deterministic factoring bound had a fixed positive exponent, the best being $N^{1/5+o(1)}$. In October 2026 Rudich and Rousseau used the new $\#$SAT counting engines to prove in Lean that one Turing machine computes the least prime factor of an $n$-bit integer in time $2^{o(n)}$. They conjectured the rate $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ as a Lean proposition, SqrtLogLogConjecture. Shunia proved this rate on paper, conditional on two unrefereed preprints, and a public repository (1337isnot1337/integer-factoring, 30 October 2026) proved it in Lean using Rudich and Rousseau's multiplication network. However, we know of only this one machine-checked proof. We present a second one, built independently along a different route. Our multiplication network is a two-dimensional Schönhage-Strassen transform over $\mathbb{Z}/(2^J+1)$ with power-of-two parameters and no twiddle factors. Its value is the number of divisors of $N$ with a given binary prefix, its shared bits total at most $2^{15}(n+2)$, and each of its $g$ nodes holds $O(n/g+n^{3/4})$ bits, so Anthropic's counting theorem evaluates it in $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ steps. We prove in Lean 4 (minFac_TM_rate; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) that a finite-alphabet Mathlib Turing machine outputs $\mathrm{minFac}(N)$ within $\lfloor C\cdot 2^{Cn/\sqrt{\log_2(n+2)}}\rfloor$ steps for every $N$. From this we derive Rudich and Rousseau's SqrtLogLogConjecture, in their definitions copied verbatim and with constant $2C$, and their headline theorem. We claim no priority for the rate or for a formal proof, and we have not rebuilt the other development. The constants are astronomically large, the machine uses exponential space, and the full factorisation is not formalised. The proof rests on Anthropic's Lean development of its claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, which is under review.
