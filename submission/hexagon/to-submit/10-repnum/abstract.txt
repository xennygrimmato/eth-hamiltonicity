For a fixed positive-definite integer matrix $A$, the representation number $r_A(n)=\#\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^k : x^{\top}Ax=n\}$ is classical. Four ternary representation numbers decide Tunnell's congruent-number test, and through Waldspurger's theorem such numbers give central values of twisted $L$-functions. However, for a general ternary form the best deterministic bound we know for a single $r_A(n)$ is $n^{1/2+o(1)}$, and we know of no bound of the form $n^{o(1)}$, even conditionally. We present a deterministic algorithm that outputs $r_A(n)$ in time $2^{O(L/\sqrt{\log L})}=n^{o(1)}$, where $L$ is the bit length of $n$, for every fixed positive-definite $A$ of any dimension. Our approach shifts $x$ into a box of naturals by an adjugate Cauchy-Schwarz bound, splits signs so that $Q(u-B)=n$ becomes an identity between two nonnegative straight-line programs, pads the input so that all values fit, and counts the solutions with Anthropic's counting engine through our earlier network library. We prove three theorems in Lean 4 (repNum_TM_rate, diagRep_TM_rate, tunnell_TM_rate; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound): a finite-alphabet Mathlib Turing machine outputs the general count, the diagonal count, and Tunnell's test bit within $\lfloor C\cdot 2^{CL/\sqrt{\log_2(L+2)}}\rfloor$ steps. On paper, Tunnell's Theorem 3 then gives $L(E_n,1)$ exactly for squarefree $n$, and a theorem of Bocherer and Schulze-Pillot gives central values of imaginary quadratic twists of a fixed curve of squarefree conductor, in time $n^{o(1)}$ and $|D|^{o(1)}$ respectively; if OpenAI's unrefereed full-BSD result holds, the counts also give the order of the Tate-Shafarevich group of $E_n$ in analytic rank 0. The constants are astronomical, the machines use exponential space, and everything rests on Anthropic's Lean development of its claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, which is under review.
