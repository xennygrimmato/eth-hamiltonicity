# Ni (15 Nov 2026): exact π(N) and prime finding in N^{o(1)}, and what it means for our claims

**Source.** J. Ni, *Exact prime counting and deterministic prime finding in time N^{o(1)}*. Announcement dated
15 Nov 2026, alphaXiv "2611.sublinear-prime-counting-deterministic-finding"; code at
github.com/6teamFurina/prime-algorithm, tag v0.1-verified. It is unrefereed. The author reports a Lean 4.33.1 proof
against a Mathlib-only spec, checked with Comparator and nanoda, and asks for independent reproduction. It builds on
Anthropic's 3-SAT formalisation and on **our** library (commit 4193596). It cites our parity paper (2611.00116) and
our barrier paper (2611.00149).

**Claims [K, not yet reproduced by us].** S(N) = Σ_{p≤N} p, π(N), and a prime in (N, 2N], each in
2^{O(b/√log b)} deterministic time. This settles the weak Polymath4 goal and TCH Conjecture 1.1, in a stronger form.

**How it escapes our barriers.**
1. **No factoring, no iterated product.** It uses 1_prime(n) = −μ(n)Λ(n)/log n with Λ(n) = Σ_{dm=n} μ(d) log m. Both
   Möbius values come from **Ramanujan sums**, μ(d) = Σ_{0≤x<d, (x,d)=1} e(x/d). Coprimality is certified by a unique
   bounded Bézout witness (as in our w1 I5).
2. **Analytic weights, then rounding.** e(·), log and 1/log at rational points are replaced by Cauchy-kernel
   (trapezoidal-on-a-circle) quadratures with M = O(b) nodes. Each kernel value is an integer quotient
   ⌊2^t 2^p q|X|/D⌋, and the products of four quotients become multiplicities. The total error is below 1/2, so
   rounding recovers S(N) exactly.
3. **One network.** It has 72 variables, 33 equations and 132 product slots, with O(b) shared bits. The queries are
   polynomially many, and the exponent depends only on the shared bits of a single system.

**Corrections to our own notes.**
- **p3 notebook §8, "Möbius on full factors is not an engine primitive" [was C]: FALSE.** Ramanujan sums plus
  approximate phases make it one.
- **node "Only doors left" and p1/p3/node "exact counts need the iterated product": scope too wide.** These are
  correct only for integer divisor-product networks, which the tree-model bounds cover. Ni's route uses real-valued
  analytic weights with exact final rounding, and none of our lower bounds apply to it.
- **Barrier paper 2611.00149, Table "Nine problems out of reach", rows "prime finding" and "exact π(x)".** Each row's
  "why" column is route-specific and the Lean theorems remain true. But the row's headline, "out of reach of the
  engines' guarantees", is overtaken if Ni holds. **A v2 or a note is needed.**
- **The linear-query barriers (p1 Thm 2, p2 syndrome, p3 pivot, w2 Prop G) are unaffected.** They concern parity or
  residue queries plus distributional facts. Ni computes exact counts.
- **p4 Theorem 7.1** (prime > u in u^{o(1)} via a Selberg certificate) is correct (review/theorem71.md) but subsumed
  by Ni's Theorem 3. Its interest is only as a second, purely Type-I route to the weak goal.

**Lesson for the programme.** We restricted ourselves to exact integer weights. The leap was to allow
approximate analytic weights (phases and logarithms as short rational-kernel sums) and to round only at the end.
