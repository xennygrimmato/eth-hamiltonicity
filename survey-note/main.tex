\title[What survives the ETH refutations?]{What survives the ETH refutations?\\ A map of fine-grained hypotheses\\ and conditional lower bounds}

\author{Vaibhav Tulsyan}

\date{November 2026}

\subjclass[2020]{68Q17, 68Q25, 68Q27, 68W40}
\keywords{Exponential Time Hypothesis, Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis, fine-grained complexity, conditional lower bounds, parameterized complexity, CNF-SAT, clause width}

\begin{abstract}
In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for
$\#k$-SAT, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) and with it the Strong ETH. This note
records, row by row, which fine-grained hypotheses and which published conditional lower bounds
are affected, assuming the claimed algorithms are correct. We classify $33$ hypotheses and $27$
conditional theorems as refuted, as having lost their premise, or as surviving; a fourth label
marks the few theorems whose conclusion is now contradicted by an explicit algorithm. The map is
organised by one distinction: the new algorithms handle $k$-CNF formulas of bounded width, and,
through the width reduction of Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi, CNFs of any width with at most
$n\cdot 2^{o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})}$ clauses (a short derivation of ours from the
explicit rate of the Anthropic manuscript), but nothing is known for CNF-SAT with polynomially
many clauses of unbounded width. Many of the best-known lower bounds (treewidth optimality,
edit distance, Fr\'echet distance, LCS, diameter approximation) are proved from that
``CNF-SETH'' and survive with their premise intact. Gap-ETH and the ETH-based proof of the
Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis lose their force; Almost-ETH, OV, 3SUM, APSP and the
Set Cover Conjecture are untouched. We list the few genuinely new algorithms that follow. A
refutation removes premises; it does not by itself give algorithms, and nothing here is
practical. Everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The two refutations}
The Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) of Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{IP99,ImpagliazzoPaturi01}
defines $s_k$, for $k\ge3$, as the infimum of the $\delta$ such that $k$-SAT on $n$ variables is
solvable in time $O(2^{\delta n})$, and asserts $s_k>0$ for $k\ge3$; the Strong ETH (SETH) is
$s_\infty=\lim_k s_k=1$~\cite{ImpagliazzoPaturi01}. In October 2026 two manuscripts appeared, by
OpenAI~\cite{OpenAI26} and by Anthropic~\cite{Anthropic26}, each claiming a deterministic algorithm
that counts the satisfying assignments of a $k$-CNF in subexponential time. Both come with Lean~4
formalisations of their top-level statements. These are \emph{claimed} results, still under
review. We write \A{} and \Oa{} for them, and use them in the following forms.

\begin{importedtheorem}[{\A, Main Theorem, eq.~(1)}]\label{imp:A}
There is an absolute constant $C$ and a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of a $k$-CNF
with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses in time
\[
  2^{C\cdot\min\{k^2 n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n},\;km/\sqrt{\log m}\}}.
\]
\end{importedtheorem}

\begin{importedtheorem}[{\Oa, main theorem}]\label{imp:O}
For each fixed $k$ there is a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of a $k$-CNF with $n$
variables and $m$ clauses in $(n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ bit operations and
polynomial space, and in $2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ when $m\le Dn$.
\end{importedtheorem}

Two features matter below. First, \cref{imp:A} is \emph{uniform in $k$}: $C$ is absolute, so the
bound is subexponential in $n$ as long as $k=o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})$. \Oa{} states
no dependence on $k$, so it is not known to be uniform. Uniformity is irrelevant for ETH and SETH:
each engine gives $s_k=0$ for every fixed $k$, hence $s_\infty=0$ (\A, Corollary~5; the reduction
from $3$-SAT to $k$-SAT is the deterministic sparsification of~\cite{IPZ01}). It matters only for
formulas whose width grows with $n$ (\cref{sec:width}). Second, both algorithms are deterministic
and count, so they refute the randomized, counting and nondeterministic variants of ETH and SETH
\emph{for $k$-CNFs} at once (\A, Corollaries~5 and~6 and the remark after Corollary~5).

\subsection{The direction of implication}
A conditional lower bound ``$H\Rightarrow$ problem $X$ needs time $T$'' is proved by a reduction
\emph{from} a problem covered by $H$ \emph{to} $X$. Refuting $H$ removes the premise; it says
nothing about $X$. New algorithms for $X$ arise only when there is a reduction in the other
direction, from $X$ to $k$-SAT with a linear number of variables (the SERF framework
of~\cite{IPZ01}, and \A, Corollary~7), or from more general theorems inside the manuscripts that
the top-level statements do not expose (our own sparse results, \cref{sec:new}). Most rows below
therefore lose a premise and gain nothing. We say so row by row.

\subsection{Statuses}\label{sec:statuses}
Throughout, ``refuted'' means \emph{refuted assuming the claimed refutations}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{refuted}: the hypothesis itself is false.
\item \textbf{premise refuted}: a theorem $H\Rightarrow X$ with $H$ false; nothing here contradicts $X$.
\item \textbf{survives}: we know of no consequence of the refutations that touches the statement.
For a theorem this means that its premise, as actually used in the proof, still stands.
\end{itemize}
The additional label \textbf{conclusion refuted} marks a theorem $H\Rightarrow X$ whose conclusion
$X$ is contradicted by an explicit algorithm. One row is a theorem whose premise is the
\emph{negation} of ETH, so that the theorem now \textbf{fires} (\cref{sec:circuits}); a few rows
are \textbf{mixed} and are split in the reason column.

\subsection{Sources}
The map was compiled from the primary sources: for every row tagged \Lit{} in
\cref{tab:hyp,tab:thm} the wording was checked against the abstract or full text of the cited
source (arXiv, ECCC, DROPS, journal, or authors' copy). Rows tagged \Fol{} are folklore
conjectures without a single primary statement; \Inf{} marks our own inference, and \Own{} our own
results. In an earlier pass, nine rows rested on secondary sources or on sources we had not
re-read (ETH itself; the CIP duality; Williams' Max-2-CSP algorithm; Feige's hypothesis;
P\u{a}tra\c{s}cu--Williams, twice; Cai--Juedes; Chen--Huang--Kanj--Xia; Marx's treewidth paper).
All nine were re-verified against the primary text or abstract for this note, so no row now
rests on a secondary source. Theorem numbers refer to the versions named in the bibliography.

\section{Bounded width versus unbounded width}\label{sec:width}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Three facts}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(F\arabic*)}]
\item\label{F1} $s_k=0$ for every $k$. This refutes ETH, SETH, $\#$ETH, $\#$SETH, $\oplus$SETH and
NSETH in their $k$-CNF forms.
\item\label{F2} \emph{Local witnesses.} A problem with complexity parameter $n$ whose yes-instances
have an $O(n)$-bit witness checkable by an $O(n)$-gate circuit, or by bounded-width clauses on
$O(n)$ variables, gets deterministic time $2^{\delta n}$ for every $\delta>0$ by Tseitin's
transformation; counts follow when the encoding is parsimonious. This is the SERF
route~\cite{IPZ01}; \A, Corollary~7, uses it with a cardinality circuit of $O(n)$ gates.
\item\label{F3} \emph{The width frontier} (\cref{prop:frontier} below). CNFs of arbitrary width are
reached only when the number of clauses is barely superlinear.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The width reduction and the density frontier}
Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{CIP06} relate CNF-SAT of bounded density to $k$-SAT by
Schuler's width reduction: while some clause $C$ has more than $k$ literals, pick $k$ of them,
$C'$, and branch on ``$C'$ is satisfied'' (replace $C$ by $C'$) versus ``$C'$ is falsified'' (set
its $k$ literals to false). The branches are disjoint, so model counts add up, and the leaves are
$k$-CNFs. This counting form is \cite[Lemma~6.1]{TulsyanQ26}; with constant $k$ it gives
$\#$CNF-SAT with $m\le cn$ clauses in time $2^{\varepsilon n}$ for all $c,\varepsilon>0$, from
either engine~\cite[Corollary~6.2]{TulsyanQ26}. The uniformity of \cref{imp:A} lets $k$ grow, and
gives a little more.

\begin{proposition}[our derivation from \cref{imp:A}]\label{prop:frontier}
Put $L(n)=(\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n}$. Assume \cref{imp:A}. For every function $f$ with
$f(n)=o(L(n))$ there is a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of CNF formulas of
arbitrary width with $n$ variables and at most $n\cdot2^{f(n)}$ clauses in time $2^{o(n)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\le Dn$ with $\log D=f(n)$, and fix $k\ge2$, to be chosen. Run the width reduction. Along a
root-to-leaf path every ``satisfied'' branch removes one long clause, so there are at most $m$ of
them, and every ``falsified'' branch fixes $k$ variables, so there are $r\le n/k$ of them. The
leaves with exactly $r$ falsified branches number at most $\binom{m+r}{r}$~\cite[proof of
Lemma~5]{CIP06}, so there are at most
$(n/k+1)\binom{m+\lfloor n/k\rfloor}{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\le(n+1)\bigl(e(Dk+1)\bigr)^{n/k}$ leaves.
Each leaf is a $k$-CNF with $n'\le n$ variables and at most $m$ clauses. If $n'\le\sqrt n$, count
by brute force in time $2^{\sqrt n}\poly(m)$. Otherwise $\log n'\ge\frac12\log n$, so
$\log\log n'/\sqrt{\log n'}\le2\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ for $n$ large, and \cref{imp:A} counts
the leaf in time $2^{s n}$ with $s=2Ck^2\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$. The total time is at most
\[
  \poly(n,m)\cdot2^{\,(n/k)\,(f(n)+\log(k+1)+2)\;+\;sn\;+\;\sqrt n}.
\]
Take $k=\lceil\sqrt{\max\{f(n),1\}\cdot L(n)}\,\rceil$. Then $k\to\infty$, $f(n)/k\le\sqrt{f(n)/L(n)}\to0$
and $(\log k)/k\to0$, while $k/L(n)\to0$ gives $s=2C(k/L(n))^2\to0$. Every term of the exponent
is $o(n)$, and $\poly(n,m)=2^{o(n)}$ since $m\le n^{1+o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what does not follow]\label{rem:frontier}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item The clause-count branch of \cref{imp:A} does worse here: it needs $km=o(n\sqrt{\log m})$,
which allows only $m\le n(\log n)^{1/2-o(1)}$.
\item Through \Oa{} only constant density is reached explicitly, since its $O_k$-constants are not
stated; a diagonal argument gives some unbounded but non-explicit density.
\item For $m=n^{1+\Omega(1)}$, or $m\ge n\cdot2^{(\log n)^{1/4+\Omega(1)}}$, nothing follows: the
leaf count forces $k\gtrsim\log(m/n)$, where neither engine is subexponential in $n$. Hence the
statement
\begin{center}
\emph{CNF-SETH}: for every $\varepsilon>0$, CNF-SAT with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses of
unbounded width is not solvable in time $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\poly(m)$
\end{center}
survives, together with its counting, parity and nondeterministic forms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\noindent CNF-SETH is exactly the premise that many famous reductions use. Lokshtanov, Marx and
Saurabh~\cite{LMS11tw} assume that ``SAT cannot be solved in $(2-\varepsilon)^n m^{O(1)}$ time''
and stress that they need no sparsification; Backurs and Indyk~\cite{BI18} reduce from CNF-SAT
with $M$ clauses to edit distance; Bringmann's ``SETH$'$''~\cite{Bri14} is ``no $O^*((2-\delta)^N)$
algorithm for CNF-SAT''; Roditty and Vassilevska Williams~\cite[Theorem~1]{RVW13} conclude
``CNF-SAT on $n$ variables is in $O^*((2-\delta)^n)$''; and the moderate-dimension Orthogonal
Vectors conjecture follows from CNF-SETH by Williams' split-and-list reduction with $d=m$. What
dies is everything that genuinely goes through sparsification to $O(n)$ clauses, or through
bounded width.

\subsection{Circuit lower bounds}\label{sec:circuits}
Williams~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{Wil13} proved that if ETH is false then $\ENP$ has no circuits of
linear size; \A{} draws this consequence and formalises it in Lean. Modulo the refutations,
$\ENP\not\subseteq\mathrm{SIZE}(O(n))$ is therefore unconditional. In a companion
note~\cite{TulsyanC26} we track the constants through Williams' argument and the local reductions
of Jahanjou, Miles and Viola~\cite{JMV15}: assuming the clause-count bound of \cref{imp:A} in a
Turing-machine form, there are $\varepsilon>0$ and $L_0\in\ENP$ with no circuits of size
$\varepsilon n\sqrt{\log n}$ for infinitely many $n$. That result is machine-checked in Lean on top of
Anthropic's own circuit-lower-bound module (\texttt{enp\_not\_size\_n\_sqrt\_log}; standard axioms;
fresh kernel replay), holds only infinitely often, and has a non-explicit $\varepsilon$. It gives nothing at $n\log n$ and nothing for NEXP. Carmosino et
al.~\cite{CGIMPS16} note that disproving NSETH gives circuit lower bounds; for the $k$-CNF form
refuted here this is subsumed by the above.

\section{The new algorithms}\label{sec:new}
% =====================================================================

The refutations give few explicit algorithms beyond $k$-SAT itself. We list all we know of,
separating what needs only the formalised top-level statements from what needs internal results
of \A.

\subsection{From the main theorems alone}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $k$-SAT, $\#k$-SAT, $\oplus k$-SAT and $k$-TAUT; $\#$CNF-SAT of any width with $O(n)$ clauses
(either engine), and with $n2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses (\A; \cref{prop:frontier}); linear-size Circuit-SAT.
\item \A, Corollary~7: for every $\delta>0$, deterministic $O(2^{\delta n})$ algorithms that count
the independent sets, cliques, vertex covers and dominating sets of each size $s$, and the proper
$k$-colourings, of a graph on $n$ vertices with any number of edges. Decision for IS, Clique, VC
and $k$-Colouring was implied before by~\cite{IPZ01}; Dominating Set and the counts are new
in~\A.
\item $k$-Hitting Set, $k$-Set Splitting, $k$-NAE-SAT, $\#2$-SAT, sparse permanent, $\#$PM, Boolean
$\#$CSP, and the integer Potts and flow hyperbolas of the Tutte plane on sparse graphs (local
encodings, \ref{F2}).
\item \emph{Vertex Cover in $2^{o(k)}+\poly(n)$} (our observation). The LP kernel leaves at most $2k$
vertices in polynomial time~\cite[\S2.5]{CyganEtAl15}; Corollary~7 then counts vertex covers of
size $k$ in $2^{2\delta k}$ for every $\delta$. A single algorithm in time
$2^{O(k\log\log k/\sqrt{\log k})}+\poly(n)$ follows by running the explicit $n$-bound of
\cref{imp:A} on the $O(k)$-variable $3$-CNF of the proof of Corollary~7. This contradicts the
conclusion of Cai--Juedes' ETH bound (B14), consistently with their own equivalence.
\item \emph{Sparse Max-Cut and Max-$k$-SAT, unweighted} (our observation). For $m=O(n)$ constraints
with $O(1)$-bit weights, encode each constraint by a forced indicator variable, add an $O(m)$-gate
adder and a comparison with a target $t$, and apply Tseitin: a $3$-CNF with $O(n)$ variables whose
models are the assignments of value exactly $t$. Corollary~6 counts them for each $t$, giving the
exact optimum and the number of solutions of each value in $2^{\delta n}$. Weights up to $W$ cost
$O(m\log W)$ gates, so polynomial weights are not covered by the main theorems.
\item $\mathrm{M}[1]$-complete problems in FPT time (A15).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{From internal results of \texorpdfstring{\A}{[A]}}
Our other results assume three internal statements of \A{} (a network-to-clique reduction, an
algorithm for cliques of all levels, and the existence of its ``pieces''), called \HA{}
in~\cite{Tulsyan26,TulsyanQ26}. They are not consequences of the top-level theorems.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},resume]
\item \emph{Sparse Hamiltonicity}~\cite{Tulsyan26}: assuming \HA, the number of Hamiltonian cycles of
a graph with $n$ vertices and $O(n)$ edges in $O(2^{\delta n})$ for every $\delta$, and in
$2^{O(\max(m,n)/\sqrt{\log n})}$ in general; hence decision, search, Hamiltonian paths, sparse TSP
with polynomial weights, directed cycles and ATSP. These theorems are machine-checked in Lean on top
of the development of \A, relative to \HA.
\item \emph{Exact quantum amplitudes}~\cite{TulsyanQ26}: assuming \HA, amplitudes of Clifford+T
circuits with $G$ two-qubit gates in $2^{O(G/\sqrt{\log G})}$, machine-checked relative to \HA;
marginals and exact sampling on paper.
\item \emph{Further sparse problems, on paper only}~\cite{TulsyanRepo26}: Steiner Tree, FVS,
Connected VC and DS, Max-Leaf; the line $T(x,1)$ of the Tutte plane (forests counted by size) on
sparse graphs, which is the line Brand, Dell and Roth~\cite{BrandDellRoth19} show $\#$ETH-hard;
sparse Max-Cut with polynomial weights. All are conditional on \HA{} and not formalised.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What gets no algorithm}
Nothing parameterised by treewidth or pathwidth; no problem in P (edit distance, LCS, Fr\'echet,
diameter, OV, 3SUM, APSP, $k$-Clique); Set Cover by universe size; Subset Sum; dense Max-CSP;
dense Hamiltonicity; planar problems (where $2^{O(\sqrt n)}$ is already better).

\section{Reading the map by area}\label{sec:areas}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Exact and parameterised algorithms}
The treewidth and pathwidth bounds (B1, B2) are the clearest case of a premise that survives
because of how it is stated. Lokshtanov, Marx and Saurabh build, from a CNF formula with $n$
variables and $m$ clauses, a graph of size polynomial in $n+m$ and treewidth roughly $n/\log_2 c$
(the precise form varies by problem),
so a $(c-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{tw}}$ algorithm would decide CNF-SAT in time
$2^{(1-\delta)n}\poly(m)$. Nothing in the reduction requires bounded width or few clauses, and
\cref{rem:frontier} shows that the new algorithms reach only $m\le n2^{o(L(n))}$. To refute B1
one would need CNF-SAT with, say, $n^2$ clauses of width up to $n$ in time $2^{(1-\delta)n}$; the
width reduction would then need $k\approx\log n$, where the bound of \cref{imp:A} is
$2^{\Theta(n(\log n)^{3/2}\log\log n)}$, far above $2^n$.

The ETH-based bounds for parameterised problems (B19--B22) lose their premise, but are not
contradicted: a $2^{\delta n}$ algorithm says nothing about $f(k)n^{o(k)}$ when $k\ll n$. The two
exceptions are structural. Chen and Grohe's equivalence (A15) is an ``if and only if'', so its
other direction now yields $\mathrm{M}[1]=\mathrm{FPT}$. And Vertex Cover parameterised by solution
size has a linear kernel, so the parameter and the number of vertices coincide after
preprocessing, and the $2^{\delta n}$ algorithm of \A{} becomes a $2^{o(k)}$ algorithm (B14).
Whether $\mathrm{W}[1]\ne\mathrm{FPT}$ (A16) is unaffected: Chen and Grohe list its equivalence
with ETH as open, and the refutations settle ETH without touching it.

\subsection{Approximation}
Gap-ETH is refuted by any exact $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm, so it falls with ETH; the gap does not help.
The FPT-inapproximability results of Chalermsook et al.\ (B23) therefore lose their premise, and
so do the ETH-hardness results of Dinur--Manurangsi, Braverman--Ko--Weinstein and
Aaronson--Impagliazzo--Moshkovitz (B24) and the ETH proof of PIH (B25). Two kinds of result
survive. The first are the re-proofs under $\mathrm{W}[1]\ne\mathrm{FPT}$: constant-factor
$k$-Clique (Lin), $k^{1/H(k)}$-factor $k$-Clique (Karthik--Khot) and $F(k)$-factor $k$-DomSet
(Karthik--Laekhanukit--Manurangsi). The second are results whose reductions are only mildly
exponential, so that a weaker hypothesis suffices: Manurangsi's Densest-$k$-Subgraph bound, now
stated under Almost-ETH (A33). The refutations give $3$-SAT in $2^{n/(\log n)^{c}}$ for every constant
$c<1/2$, which is $2^{n^{1-o(1)}}$ but not $2^{n^{1-\gamma}}$, so Almost-ETH stands. We expect
other quasi-polynomial-size reductions (Nash, free games, 2-CSP) to give weaker but non-trivial
bounds under Almost-ETH; we have not checked this (\Inf).

\subsection{Problems in P}
Here the map is almost entirely ``survives''. Edit distance, Fr\'echet distance and diameter are
stated from CNF-SAT with $\poly(m)$ clauses (B5, B7, B8); LCS and DTW go through Most-OV or OV
with dimension $d=m$ (B6), which CNF-SETH implies. What is lost is the derivation of the
\emph{low-dimension} OV conjecture ($d=c\log n$) from SETH, which uses sparsification to $O(n)$
clauses (A17, B10). OVC itself, 3SUM, APSP and the hyperclique hypothesis were never consequences
of ETH. The dynamic lower bounds of Abboud and Vassilevska Williams (B9) are a borderline case:
stated through sparsification, but by our reading proved by constructions that also run from
CNF-SETH. The barrier of Carmosino et al.\ (B26), that NSETH forbids deterministic fine-grained
reductions from SAT to 3SUM and APSP, disappears for $k$-SAT; no such reduction is known.

\subsection{Counting and quantum}
Counting statements behave like decision ones: $\#$ETH and the $k$-CNF form of $\#$SETH fall
(A6, A7), and lower bounds proved by local reductions on sparse instances are contradicted by
explicit algorithms (B17): Corollaries~6 and~7 of \A{} count independent sets and models of
$2$-CNFs at any density, and local encodings handle sparse permanents, perfect matchings and the
integer hyperbolas of the Tutte plane. The non-local cases (Hamiltonian cycles, forests) need
\HA{}. Dense permanents, and the Tutte plane on dense graphs, are untouched. For the quantum
statements (B18) the same split appears: sparse Clifford+T circuits fall under \HA, while dense
or average-case conjectures survive~\cite[\S6]{TulsyanQ26}.

Three points stand out. First, the most-cited SETH lower bounds, for treewidth DP and for edit

algorithms in clause density is sharp in an unusual way: $n\cdot2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses through the
explicit rate of \A, constant density through \Oa, and nothing at $n^{1+\Omega(1)}$.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{Everything is conditional} on the claimed refutations~\cite{Anthropic26,OpenAI26},
whose expert review is pending. If they fail, every ``refuted'' becomes ``open'' and every
``premise refuted'' reverts to a valid conditional theorem; \cref{prop:frontier} and the
observations of \cref{sec:new} lose their proofs. Results marked \HA{} need internal statements
of \A{} that are not part of its formalised top level.
\item \emph{Direction.} A refutation removes premises. Except through \ref{F2} and the network
theorems, it gives no algorithm, and ``premise refuted'' is never evidence that a conclusion is
false.
\item \emph{Sources.} Every row was checked against its primary source; the nine rows that earlier
rested on secondary sources were re-verified for this note (\cref{sec:intro}), and no row is now
secondary. Rows marked \Fol{} have no single primary statement; rows marked \Inf{} are our
inferences and are argued only in the reason column. B8 omits later diameter tradeoffs. The map is
not exhaustive: \A{} reports more than $800$ papers with results proved only under ETH or a
hypothesis implying it.
\item \emph{Proofs.} \Cref{prop:frontier} and the observations (iv)--(v) of \cref{sec:new} are short
written arguments, produced with AI assistance and not machine-checked or reviewed by a human
expert. Only the results so labelled in \cref{sec:new} are machine-checked, and only relative to
their stated hypotheses.
\item \emph{Nothing practical.} \A{} notes that its bound does not beat $2^n$ for $3$-CNFs with
$5n$ clauses unless $n>10^{10\,000}$. All algorithms here are galactic.
\end{enumerate}
