# Ideas: P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ (Blum–Shub–Smale) after VP ≠ VNP

24 Nov 2026. Idea-generation only; nothing here is executed or Lean-checked.

Tags: **[K]** known, source cited (statement read in the source unless marked "from memory").
**[T26]** claimed 2026 theorem we take as input (border VP ≠ VNP etc.).
**[R]** derived here, proof sketch given, *not* checked beyond the sketch. **[C]** conjecture / open.

Notation. L = straight-line complexity over ℂ (constants free), L̄ = border (approximative) complexity,
C(g) = algebraic-computation-tree decision complexity of {g = 0}, and
**EC(g) = min{ L(f) : f ≠ 0, g | f }** (exclusion complexity, Bürgisser 2004).
P_ℂ(nu), NP_ℂ(nu) = nonuniform classes (poly-size algebraic circuits with selector gates, arbitrary constants).
Uniform P_ℂ = NP_ℂ implies P_ℂ(nu) = NP_ℂ(nu), so **P_ℂ(nu) ≠ NP_ℂ(nu) ⇒ P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ**; all routes below aim at the nonuniform statement.

## 0. Verdict

1. **No Nov 2026 claim of P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ or of the τ-conjecture was found.** The state of the art is Bürgisser,
   arXiv:2606.25121v2 (19 Oct 2026), which proves the *opposite* direction (BSS separation ⇒ constant-free Valiant
   separation) and states exactly the implication we want as **Conjecture 1.1: VNP_ℂ ⊄ V̄P_ℂ ⇒ P_ℂ(nu) ≠ NP_ℂ(nu)**.
   The hypothesis of that conjecture is now [T26]. So the target is "prove Bürgisser's Conjecture 1.1".
2. **No known implication "P_ℂ = NP_ℂ ⇒ VP = VNP" (any form).** We are *not* done by contraposition. The known
   theorem is the converse: VP⁰ = VNP⁰ ⇒ P⁰_ℂ(nu) = NP⁰_ℂ(nu).
3. **The gap is one precise thing: hardness of *all nonzero multiples* of an exponential-degree polynomial.**
   P_ℂ(nu) = NP_ℂ(nu) gives, by the generic path, a small circuit for *some multiple* Q = R·H of the resultant R
   (or of a "Boolean norm" N_g = ∏_{e∈{0,1}^n}(y − g(x,e))). VP ≠ VNP gives that R and N_g *themselves* are hard
   ([BCJS26] via Bürgisser; §2 (R1)). The cofactor H is the whole problem. Branching and constants are *not* the
   obstacle (generic path + working over ℂ dispose of both); exponential degree and the cofactor are.
4. **Two things the border bound does give immediately** (both [R], both ≤ 3 h to check): **P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ**
   (Bürgisser 2004 Cor. 1.5 + border bound: the graph of the permanent is not in P_ℂ(nu)), and a **barrier**:
   every hypersurface of polynomial degree in NP_ℂ(nu) is border-easy, so any generic-path proof of P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ must
   use hypersurfaces of superpolynomial degree. This kills the "find a low-degree hard polynomial with NP_ℂ-checkable
   graph" route unconditionally and gives graph(per) ∉ NP_ℂ(nu).
5. **Top two:** Idea 1 (low-degree theorems: P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ, graph(per) ∉ NP_ℂ(nu), the barrier) for certain value;
   Idea 2 (the cofactor problem for Boolean norms, with the "cofactor is itself border-hard" lemma as first step)
   for the actual target.

Nothing Boolean is known to *follow* from P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ (no known "P = NP ⇒ P_ℂ = NP_ℂ"; the closest is B8, which
needs VP⁰(u) = VNP⁰(u)). So an unconditional proof is not known to imply any Boolean separation; the only known
consequence is VP⁰(u) ≠ VNP⁰(u), which [T26] presumably already gives. No barrier from that side.

### 1.2 What is now a theorem, and what it buys

- [T26] VNP ⊄ V̄P over every field, L̄(per_m) ≥ exp(c(log m)^{4/3}) (Anthropic App. N, Thm N.5).
- With B9: L(R_n) is superpolynomial. With (R1): L(N_g) is superpolynomial for suitable g.
- With B11: **C(graph(per_n)) and C({per_n = 0}) are superpolynomial** ⇒ P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ, also P_ℝ ≠ PAR_ℝ, also against
  randomized trees (R2). This is the converse-type complement of B7 and was open (B7 says it needed VPSPACE-hardness).
- B2 and B8 become vacuous (their hypotheses/conclusions are settled); they give nothing about factorials or HN.

### 1.3 Why VP ≠ VNP does not suffice — refined

The user's understanding ("machines branch on equality tests and use arbitrary constants; the known sufficient
condition is about integers") should be corrected as follows.

- *Branching is not the obstacle.* Generic path: if A ∈ P_ℂ(nu) has closure containing a hypersurface Z(g), the
  product of the ≤ s test polynomials on the path of a generic input vanishes on Z(g); so some test is a nonzero
  multiple of each irreducible factor of g (B11, Lemma 1.1).
- *Constants are not the obstacle.* L, L̄, EC are over ℂ; [T26] holds with arbitrary constants; B4 for uniform.
- *Integers are not essential.* Shub–Smale is the special case A = {1..k} ⊂ ℂ; the integer statement arises only
  after eliminating constants and evaluating the exclusion polynomial at an integer.
- **The obstacles are:** (i) *degree* — sets in NP_ℂ that are not obviously in P_ℂ have closure of exponential
  degree, and B11 loses a polynomial in the degree; (ii) *multiples* — the machine yields Q = g·H with H
  uncontrolled; for exponential degree, a multiple can be exponentially easier than a factor
  (X^{2^n} − 1 versus a random half of its linear factors); (iii) *∃ versus Σ* — VNP is exponential summation,
  NP_ℂ is existential projection; a sum is recovered from a product ∏_e(1 + t·g_e) (derivative at t = 0) only if one
  has the product itself, not a multiple.

### 1.4 The exact remaining gap

For a p-family g_n(x,e) with poly-size circuits put N_g(x,y) = ∏_{e∈{0,1}^n}(y − g_n(x,e)). Its zero set is in
(digital) NP_ℂ: guess e with e_i² = e_i.

- (R0) [R] P_ℂ(nu) = NP_ℂ(nu) ⇒ EC(N_g) = n^{O(1)} for every such g. (Generic path; each factor y − g(x,e) is
  irreducible and must divide a test; the product of the tests has complexity ≤ 2s.) Likewise EC(R_n) = n^{O(1)}.
- (R1) [R] L(N_g) ≥ Ω(L̄-type complexity of S_g := Σ_e g(x,e)): put y = −1/t; F(x,t) = ∏_e(1 + t·g_e) has F(x,0) = 1
  and ∂_tF(x,0) = S_g; a circuit for F gives one of 3× the size for S_g (arithmetic mod t²), and truncation to
  deg S_g is polynomial. With S_g = per (Ryser-type g) and [T26], L(N_g) is superpolynomial. N_g is a specialisation
  of R (Poisson formula: Res(e_i² − e_ie_0, …, f) = ∏_e f(1,e), auxiliary variables for gates), which is presumably B9.
- **Gap G:** show **EC(N_g) is superpolynomial for one such g** (or EC(R_n)). Then P_ℂ(nu) ≠ NP_ℂ(nu). Chain of
  sufficient hypotheses, each implying the target, weakest last:
  factorial multiples hard (B1) ⇒ [K] Pochhammer multiples hard (the step inside Shub–Smale);
  Knapsack-norm multiples hard (g = Σ x_ie_i); **N_g-multiples hard for one VNP-universal g**; R_n-multiples hard
  (R_n-multiples easy ⇒ N_g-multiples border-easy by specialisation + trailing coefficient, [R]).
  The τ-conjecture is thus far stronger than needed, and the N_g form is the one where [T26] supplies hardness.
- Sanity constraints any proof of Gap G must respect: (a) char 2: ∏_{e∈F_2^n}(y − ⟨x,e⟩) is a linearised polynomial
  (Moore determinant), poly-size; so Knapsack-norm hardness is false in char 2 and no argument that is blind to the
  characteristic or to the additive-group structure of the root set can prove it; (b) root sets that are
  multiplicative groups (∏(y − ζ^j) = y^D − 1) are easy; (c) local expansions at t = 0 carry no information (N is a
  unit in ℂ[x][[t]], so every power series is a "multiple"); the constraint is global polynomiality of the cofactor.

## 2. Attack ideas

### Idea 1 — Low degree: what trees + border give now (P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ, graph(per) ∉ NP_ℂ(nu), a barrier)  [rank 1]

**Claims.**
- (R2) [R, two lines from B11 + T26] graph(per_n) = {(X,y): y = per X} and {per X = 0} are not in P_ℂ(nu); both are
  in PAR_ℂ (Ryser, polynomial depth). So **P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ**, P_ℝ ≠ PAR_ℝ, also for randomized two-sided-error trees.
- (R3) [R, sketch — the thing to check] **Lemma L.** Let A ∈ NP_ℂ(nu) via a size-s verifier, and let Z(h) be an
  irreducible hypersurface of degree d in which A is dense. Then L̄(h) ≤ poly(s,d) (exact L in the generically reduced
  case). *Sketch:* some accepting leaf set {q_1 = … = q_r = 0, q' ≠ 0} ⊂ ℂ^{n+m} has an irreducible component W
  dominating Z(h). Cut the fibres by generic linear equations in w to make W → Z(h) generically finite. A generic
  line a + s·b meets Z(h) in d simple points s_j; above each choose *one* point of W (advice: d complex vectors).
  If W is a reduced component, (s_j, w_j) is a nonsingular zero of a square system of generic combinations of the
  q_i plus the linear cuts, so Newton iteration over ℂ[[u]] truncated at degree d computes s_j(a+u) with poly(s,d)
  operations, and h(a+u) = const·∏_j s_j(a+u) mod degree d+1. Non-reduced W: perturb q̃ ↦ q̃ − εc and take cluster
  products (border), as in Bürgisser's treatment of exponential multiplicity; this is the technical risk.
- Consequences: with [T26], **graph(per) ∉ NP_ℂ(nu)**, hence NP_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ; and a **barrier**: no hypersurface of
  polynomial degree in NP_ℂ(nu) is border-hard, so Gap G cannot be bypassed by a low-degree hard family with
  NP_ℂ-checkable graph (this strengthens Bürgisser's "no algebraic one-way functions of bounded degree" from
  P-checkable to NP-checkable graphs). Natural continuation: Σ_k-levels, i.e. graph(per) ∉ PH_ℂ(nu) ⇒ PH_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ.

**Why.** Certain new unconditional separations in the BSS world; fixes rigorously where the difficulty is.
**Main risk.** R2: essentially none (check that P_ℂ ≠ PAR_ℂ is really open — B7's authors say so; and the degree/M(d)
bookkeeping against the quasi-polynomial bound). R3: non-reduced witness components; leaf sets with inequations;
making "advice = d solution points" legitimate in the nonuniform border model. coNP/PH extension: boundary strata
may fail to be components of easy varieties.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Write R2 in full (statement of Cor. 1.5/4.1, PAR_ℂ membership, constants). Then write
Lemma L in the reduced case with all genericity statements, test it on det (witness: kernel vector; branches =
eigenvalues along a line) and decide whether the non-reduced case reduces to Bürgisser's Thm 1.3.

### Idea 2 — The cofactor problem for Boolean norms (Bürgisser's Conjecture 1.1)  [rank 2; the real target]

**Claim to aim at.** Gap G: EC(N_g) superpolynomial for one g with S_g VNP-hard.

**Partial result to prove first (R4) [R, sketch].** *If Q = N_g·H with L(Q) ≤ s, then L̄(S_g) ≤ poly(s + L̄(H), deg S_g).*
Reason: N_g = Q/H is one division; evaluate at t = ε (shift) and use ∂_t log N_g|_{t=ε} = Σ_e g_e/(1 + εg_e) → S_g;
divisions are removed because S_g has polynomial degree. Hence, by [T26], **the cofactor of any easy multiple of a
hard norm is itself border-hard**. Corollary via B11 (Thm 1.3): H cannot be a product of polynomially many
irreducible factors of polynomial degree (with any multiplicities); it needs superpolynomially many distinct
factors or a factor of superpolynomial degree. So easy multiples can only arise from "complementary hard pairs"
(N, H), as in X^{D} − 1.

**Then.** (a) Equivariance, after Andrews–Forbes STOC 2022 (lower bounds for every nonzero element of a determinantal
ideal by group action + limits): ⟨R_n⟩ is stable under GL_n × GL_{n+1}; lowest-weight / trailing-coefficient
projections are free in border complexity, so H may be assumed a weight vector; classify which semi-invariant
cofactors survive the specialisation to Boolean systems. (b) Use that the tree must *reject* Z(H) ∖ Z(N) after
leaving the generic path: this yields a second small polynomial separating the components of H from those of N on
Z(Q), i.e. a recursive family of exclusion statements for H relative to Q; look for a potential function (number
of components, degree in y) that cannot drop fast enough. (c) Choose g so that N_g is irreducible with
non-solvable monodromy (e.g. the resultant itself, or Kayal's map z ↦ (z_1²,…,z_n², Σz_i)) to stay away from the
group-structured easy cases (a)–(b) of §1.4.

**Why.** It is exactly the stated open step; [T26] plus R4 give the first non-trivial restriction on H, and it uses
the *border* bound essentially (H is only known up to approximation).
**Main risk.** High. Hardness of multiples at exponential degree is the τ-conjecture phenomenon; char-2 and
root-of-unity examples show easy multiples exist for structured root sets; AF22 works for non-principal ideals with
a rich straightening basis, and ⟨R_n⟩ is principal with an arbitrary cofactor.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Write (R0), (R1), (R4) with full proofs (including the case H(x,0) ≡ 0 and division
elimination in the border model); verify the char-2 Moore example; list all known constructions of easy multiples
of hard products and check that each produces a border-hard cofactor (consistency test of R4).

### Idea 3 — Hitting-set / annihilator reformulation and minimal instances  [rank 3]

**Claim [R].** If there is a poly-size polynomial map G_n: ℂ^m → ℂ^N (or a poly-size-definable variety) whose image
closure V_n is proper and such that no nonzero SLP of size N^{O(1)} (unbounded degree) vanishes on V_n, then
P_ℂ(nu) ≠ NP_ℂ(nu) ("x ∈ im G" is in NP_ℂ; accepted inputs must leave the generic path). Shub–Smale is dim V = 0.
Conversely P_ℂ(nu) = NP_ℂ(nu) implies: every such V has a small annihilator — e.g. the set of evaluation vectors
of size-s circuits has a poly-size (unbounded-degree) equation, an "algebraic natural proof" in the sense of
Forbes–Shpilka–Volk / Grochow–Kumar–Saks–Saraf.
**Why.** Lets one pick the most favourable V. Minimal cases worth isolating: (i) plane curves t ↦ (p_n(t), q_n(t))
with p, q of SLP size n and degree 2^n (e.g. u^D = x, (u+1)^D = y): is some bivariate multiple of the implicit
equation of size poly(n)? (ii) Kayal's n+1 quadratics in n variables ("The complexity of the annihilating
polynomial", CCC 2009, shows the *minimal* annihilator is hard under Boolean assumptions; multiples are open).
A Kabanets–Impagliazzo generator from the hard permanent does **not** work: its cost exceeds the adversary's, and
by Lemma L any low-degree generator with NP_ℂ-checkable image is fooled-proof only trivially.
**Main risk.** It is a restatement of Gap G; the minimal cases may be as hard as the τ-conjecture (case (i) with
x = 1 is a twenty-questions set {(1+ζ)^D}) or may be easy by an unexpected identity.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Prove the claim carefully (codimension > 1 included). For case (i) with D ≤ 32 compute the
implicit equation and search for short SLPs for small multiples; decide whether (i) reduces to or from Pochhammer.

### Idea 4 — Diagonalisation ("no accelerator") for BSS machines  [rank 5]

**Assessment first.** A literal transfer fails for two identifiable reasons. (1) *Speed-up.* thm_noacc needs a
program that evaluates sums over v^H steps in v^{H−16} steps; in [A] the speed-up comes from the contracting case
(cutting theorem) plus the permanent circuit. P_ℂ = NP_ℂ gives only a polynomial *slow-down* for ∃ (the usual
reason diagonalisation does not separate P from NP; there are oracles both ways over ℝ — Emerson 1994, from
memory). (2) *Idempotence.* The contradiction is V = 1 + V in a nonzero ring. Existential quantification lives in
the Boolean semiring, where V = 1 + V has the solution V = 1; and exclusion polynomials only record *whether* a
branch vanishes, not how often.
**Idea.** Restore additivity through multiplicities: N_g vanishes at y = c to order #{e : g_e = c}, a counting
function in the (ℕ,+) valuation. Ask for an "exact-multiplicity" variant of the decision problem — a problem in a
BSS counting class (#P_ℂ / GCC of Bürgisser–Cucker) whose solution by a P_ℂ machine would give a polynomial with
*prescribed* orders of vanishing, hence N_g up to a unit, hence (R1) a contradiction. Target statement:
**P_ℂ ≠ P_ℂ^{#P_ℂ}** or FP_ℂ ≠ #P_ℂ unconditionally, with the [A] diagonal argument run on valuations.
**Why.** It is the honest analogue: [A]'s argument is about sums, and the BSS classes with sums are the counting
classes. Bürgisser's outlook (2606.25121 §6) asks for exactly this bridge (#P⁰_ℂ versus permanent).
**Main risk.** Gives a counting separation, not P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ; may already follow from Idea 1.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Locate in paper.txt (App. K, thm_noacc; Def. K.1) each use of additive cancellation; state
what fails over an idempotent semiring; check whether per ∈ FP_ℂ^{#P_ℂ} and whether Idea 1 already separates.

### Idea 5 — Integer sequences: weaken the Shub–Smale hypothesis instead of proving it  [rank 4]

**Observations.** (i) [R] "k! ultimately hard" ⇔ "no nonzero integer divisible by every prime ≤ k has τ ≤ (log k)^{O(1)}"
(raise to the k-th power by repeated squaring); so the hypothesis says that no arithmetic circuit of polylog size
is a universal factoring certificate, and B13 is the "symbolic factoring algorithm" upper bound. A proof must beat
every such algorithm: very deep. (ii) P_ℂ = NP_ℂ yields much more than an integer: a *uniform, constant-free family
of polynomial identities* Q_{n,c}(x) vanishing on all 2^n branches of every digital-NP set with code c.
**Idea.** Replace the integer statement by the polynomial one and exploit uniformity in c: the exclusion
polynomials for g and for g∘(affine change), for g_1·g_2, for g with one e_i fixed, are all easy simultaneously and
uniformly; look for a functional equation (e.g. N_n(x,y) = N_{n−1}(x',y)·N_{n−1}(x',y−x_n) for Knapsack) that forces
the family of cofactors H_n to satisfy an impossible recursion (degree or number-of-components count).
**Why.** Uses information Shub–Smale throw away; avoids number theory.
**Main risk.** Cofactors for different instances are unrelated a priori; uniformity only relates the *programs*.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Prove (i); verify the main theorem of 2610.28338 at the level of its two cited inputs;
write down exactly which simultaneous identities the uniform machine gives for the Knapsack family n ↦ n+1.

### Idea 6 — Use the collapse: compute instead of exclude  [rank 6]

**Claim [R].** P_ℂ(nu) = NP_ℂ(nu) ⇒ PH_ℂ(nu) = P_ℂ(nu). So with B11 and [T26] it suffices to exhibit a border-hard
family φ_n of polynomial degree whose graph lies in *some fixed level* of PH_ℂ(nu).
**Why.** More room than NP_ℂ: Lemma L only covers Σ_1.
**Main risk.** Probably false for the same reason as Lemma L (Idea 1's continuation would prove graph(per) ∉ PH_ℂ(nu));
Boolean-part heuristics also point that way. Its value is as the *negative* target of Idea 1.
**First step (≤ 3 h).** Try Lemma L for Π_1 and Σ_2 on the determinant-like toy cases; look up what is known about
BP(Σ_k over ℂ) and [BC04, Problem 8.2].

### Idea 7 (wild) — P_ℂ ≠ NP_ℂ from a *false* instance: exploit positive characteristic

In char 2 the Knapsack norm is easy; over F̄_p Frobenius gives easy multiples (y^{p^n} − y). The transfer principle
(B4) is per characteristic, and P_K vs NP_K is open in every characteristic. **Idea:** find a decision problem that
is provably in P over F̄_p for all p by Frobenius-type exclusion polynomials *whose SLPs are uniform in p*, lift the
SLP to ℤ, and show the lift must vanish identically in char 0 unless a τ-type identity holds; i.e. turn "easy
multiples exist mod every p" plus "[T26] holds over every field" into a rigidity statement in char 0 through
reduction mod p of a hypothetical char-0 exclusion circuit (which reduces to a char-p exclusion circuit for the
same norm for almost all p, where N_g is equally hard by [T26] but may have *classifiable* easy multiples).
**Why.** [T26] is the first lower bound available uniformly over all fields; the char-p world has a visible
mechanism for easy multiples that could be classified.
**Main risk.** Speculative; classification of easy multiples in char p is itself open.
