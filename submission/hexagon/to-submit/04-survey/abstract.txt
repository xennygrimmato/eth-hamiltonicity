Many lower bounds in exact, parameterised and fine-grained complexity are proved from the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) or the Strong ETH. In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT that refute both. However, a refuted hypothesis does not settle the theorems proved from it: what falls depends on the premise that each proof actually uses, and beyond remarks in the manuscripts we are unaware of a systematic account. We present a map of $33$ hypotheses and $27$ published conditional theorems, assuming the claimed algorithms are correct. Each row is refuted, has lost its premise, or survives; a fourth label marks the few theorems whose conclusion is now contradicted by an explicit algorithm. The map rests on one distinction. The new algorithms handle $k$-CNF formulas of bounded width and, through the width reduction of Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi, CNFs of any width with at most $n\cdot 2^{o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})}$ clauses (a short derivation of ours from the explicit rate of the Anthropic manuscript). Nothing is known for CNF-SAT with polynomially many clauses of unbounded width. Many of the best-known lower bounds (treewidth optimality, edit distance, Fréchet distance, LCS, diameter approximation) are proved from that "CNF-SETH" and survive with their premise intact. Gap-ETH and the ETH-based proof of the Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis lose their force; Almost-ETH, OV, 3SUM, APSP and the Set Cover Conjecture are untouched. We list the few new algorithms that follow. A refutation removes premises; it does not by itself give algorithms, and nothing here is practical. Everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review. The supporting notes are in our public repository, https://github.com/xennygrimmato/eth-hamiltonicity.
