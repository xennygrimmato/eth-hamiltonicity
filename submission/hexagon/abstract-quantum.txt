In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT, refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis. We record a corollary for quantum circuits. Assuming three internal results of the Anthropic manuscript, the amplitude $\langle y|C|x\rangle$ of a Clifford+T circuit with $G$ two-qubit gates in arbitrary connectivity and $G_1$ single-qubit gates can be computed exactly, as an element of $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]/\sqrt2^{\,k}$ with $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$, by one deterministic Turing machine in time $2^{O(G/\sqrt{\log G})}$ whenever the input length $L$ satisfies $\log L\le G^{0.9}$; this theorem is formally verified in Lean on top of the Anthropic manuscript's own Lean development (sources at https://github.com/xennygrimmato/eth-hamiltonicity). On paper, the same method gives exact marginals and hence exact sampling, and depth-$d$ circuits in $2^{O(dn/\sqrt{\log n})}$. Through OpenAI's tensor-contraction theorem the bounds hold in polynomial space when the number of Hadamard-type gates is $O(G)$. The proof is a sum over paths with one bit per wire segment, read as an Anthropic-type network, with a Kronecker substitution for $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ and a Galois bound on the coefficients. Conversely, constant-depth circuits with $O(n)$ gates encode $\#3$-SAT with $O(n)$ clauses, so the rate matches that of the refutations. We check the fine-grained quantum-supremacy conjectures of Morimae--Tamaki, Dalzell--Harrow--Koh--La Placa, Huang--Newman--Szegedy, Aaronson--Chen and Buhrman--Patro--Speelman against their sources and record which are refuted, which lose their premise, and which survive; a counting form of the Calabro--Impagliazzo--Paturi width reduction refutes the gap conjecture of Morimae and Tamaki's depth-scaling paper. Dense statements (cubic phases, permanents, random circuits) survive, and we explain why. Nothing here concerns approximate sampling, the constants are galactic, and everything is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review.
