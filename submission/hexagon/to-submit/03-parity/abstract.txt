Computing the prime-counting function $\pi(x)$ exactly takes $x^{1/2+o(1)}$ steps by the analytic method of Lagarias and Odlyzko, and to our knowledge its parity is known faster only on short intervals: Tao, Croot and Helfgott decide whether an interval of length at most $x^{1/2+c}$ near $x$ contains an odd number of primes in time $x^{1/2-c+o(1)}$. However, their identity needs the Mobius function $\mu(d)$ for all $d\le\sqrt x$, and we know of no bound of the form $x^{o(1)}$ for the parity, even conditionally. We present deterministic algorithms that output $\pi(x) \bmod 2$ and the parity of the number of primes in any interval $[A,B]$ in time $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}=x^{o(1)}$, where $n$ is the input bit length. Our approach replaces $\mu(d)$ by its residue modulo 2, which is the squarefree indicator again: the number of chains $d_1^2\mid m$, $d_2^2\mid d_1,\dots$ of depth $L$ is odd exactly when $m$ is squarefree, for $m<2^{2^L}$. The parity of $\pi$ on $[A,B]$ is then the parity of one count of tuples satisfying $O(\log n)$ multiplications, which Anthropic's counting engine evaluates through a network whose variables have geometrically shrinking widths. We prove both theorems in Lean 4 (piParity_TM_rate, piIntervalParity_TM_rate; axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). The result does not give deterministic prime finding or the exact value of $\pi(x)$: the parity can be even on every interval one queries, and exact counts seem to need iterated products, for whose product-tree verifiers we prove a lower bound in Lean. The constants are astronomical, and the result rests on Anthropic's Lean development of its claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, which is under review.
