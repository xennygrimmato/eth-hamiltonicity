\title[What survives the ETH refutations?]{What survives the ETH refutations?\\ A map of fine-grained hypotheses\\ and conditional lower bounds}

\author{Vaibhav Tulsyan}

\date{November 2026}

\subjclass[2020]{68Q17, 68Q25, 68Q27, 68W40}
\keywords{Exponential Time Hypothesis, Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis, fine-grained complexity, conditional lower bounds, parameterized complexity, CNF-SAT, clause width}

\begin{abstract}
Many lower bounds in exact, parameterised and fine-grained complexity are proved from the
Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) or the Strong ETH. In October 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic claimed
deterministic subexponential-time algorithms for $\#k$-SAT that refute both. However, a refuted
hypothesis does not settle the theorems proved from it: what falls depends on the premise that each
proof actually uses, and beyond remarks in the manuscripts we are unaware of a systematic account. We present a map of $33$
hypotheses and $27$ published conditional theorems, assuming the claimed algorithms are correct.
Each row is refuted, has lost its premise, or survives; a fourth label marks the few theorems whose
conclusion is now contradicted by an explicit algorithm. The map rests on one distinction. The new
algorithms handle $k$-CNF formulas of bounded width and, through the width reduction of Calabro,
Impagliazzo and Paturi, CNFs of any width with at most $n\cdot 2^{o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})}$
clauses (a short derivation of ours from the explicit rate of the Anthropic manuscript). Nothing is
known for CNF-SAT with polynomially many clauses of unbounded width. Many of the best-known lower
bounds (treewidth optimality, edit distance, Fr\'echet distance, LCS, diameter approximation) are
proved from that ``CNF-SETH'' and survive with their premise intact. Gap-ETH and the ETH-based proof
of the Parameterized Inapproximability Hypothesis lose their force; Almost-ETH, OV, 3SUM, APSP and
the Set Cover Conjecture are untouched. We list the few new algorithms that follow. A refutation
removes premises; it does not by itself give algorithms, and nothing here is practical. Everything
is conditional on the claimed refutations, which are under review. The supporting notes are in our
public repository, \url{https://github.com/xennygrimmato/eth-hamiltonicity}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

A large part of exact, parameterised and fine-grained complexity rests on two hypotheses. The
Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) of Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{IP99,ImpagliazzoPaturi01} defines
$s_k$, for $k\ge3$, as the infimum of the $\delta$ such that $k$-SAT on $n$ variables is solvable in
time $O(2^{\delta n})$, and asserts $s_k>0$ for $k\ge3$. The Strong ETH (SETH) is
$s_\infty=\lim_k s_k=1$~\cite{ImpagliazzoPaturi01}. Lower bounds proved from them range from
$2^{o(n)}$ bounds for NP-hard graph problems~\cite{IPZ01,LMS11} to quadratic bounds for problems in
P, such as edit distance~\cite{BI18}.

\subsection{The two refutations}
In October 2026 two manuscripts appeared, by OpenAI~\cite{OpenAI26} and by
Anthropic~\cite{Anthropic26}. Each claims a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying
assignments of a $k$-CNF in subexponential time. Both come with Lean~4 formalisations of their
top-level statements. These are \emph{claimed} results, still under review. We write \A{} and \Oa{}
for them, and use them in the following forms.

\begin{importedtheorem}[{\A, Main Theorem, eq.~(1)}]\label{imp:A}
There is an absolute constant $C$ and a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of a $k$-CNF
with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses in time
\[
  2^{C\cdot\min\{k^2 n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n},\;km/\sqrt{\log m}\}}.
\]
\end{importedtheorem}

\begin{importedtheorem}[{\Oa, main theorem}]\label{imp:O}
For each fixed $k$ there is a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of a $k$-CNF with $n$
variables and $m$ clauses in $(n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ bit operations and
polynomial space, and in $2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ when $m\le Dn$.
\end{importedtheorem}

Two features matter below. First, \cref{imp:A} is \emph{uniform in $k$}: $C$ is absolute, so the
bound is subexponential in $n$ as long as $k=o((\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n})$. \Oa{} states
no dependence on $k$, so it is not known to be uniform. Uniformity is irrelevant for ETH and SETH.
Each engine gives $s_k=0$ for every fixed $k$, hence $s_\infty=0$ (\A, Corollary~5; the reduction
from $3$-SAT to $k$-SAT is the deterministic sparsification of~\cite{IPZ01}). It matters only for
formulas whose width grows with $n$ (\cref{sec:width}). Second, both algorithms are deterministic
and count. So they refute the randomized, counting and nondeterministic variants of ETH and SETH
\emph{for $k$-CNFs} at once (\A, Corollaries~5 and~6 and the remark after Corollary~5).

\subsection{The problem: which results fall?}
However, a refutation of ETH does not tell us which of the results proved from it fall. A
conditional lower bound ``$H\Rightarrow$ problem $X$ needs time $T$'' is proved by a reduction
\emph{from} a problem covered by $H$ \emph{to} $X$. Refuting $H$ removes the premise; it says
nothing about $X$. Worse, the premise named in a theorem is often stronger than the premise its
proof uses. OpenAI~\cite[\S1.1.3]{OpenAI26} already observe this for several bounds: those for
Fr\'echet distance, diameter approximation and the treewidth dependence of Independent Set and
Dominating Set also follow from CNF-SAT of unbounded width, which their theorem does not reach, and
new algorithms need reductions in the other direction. Beyond such remarks we are unaware of a
systematic account of what the refutations affect, and without one every result stated ``under
ETH'' or ``under SETH'' is in doubt.

We consider the \emph{classification problem}: for each fine-grained hypothesis and each published
theorem $H\Rightarrow X$, decide what follows from \A{} and \Oa. We take the view that the status of
a theorem depends on its premise \emph{as actually used in the proof}, not as stated. Throughout,
``refuted'' means \emph{refuted assuming the claimed refutations}, and we use the following
statuses.\label{sec:statuses}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{refuted}: the hypothesis itself is false.
\item \textbf{premise refuted}: a theorem $H\Rightarrow X$ with $H$ false; nothing here contradicts $X$.
\item \textbf{survives}: we know of no consequence of the refutations that touches the statement.
For a theorem this means that its premise, as actually used in the proof, still stands.
\end{itemize}
The additional label \textbf{conclusion refuted} marks a theorem $H\Rightarrow X$ whose conclusion
$X$ is contradicted by an explicit algorithm. One row is a theorem whose premise is the
\emph{negation} of ETH, so that the theorem now \textbf{fires} (\cref{sec:circuits}). A few rows
are \textbf{mixed} and are split in the reason column.

\begin{example}[Running example]\label{ex:running}
Lokshtanov, Marx and Saurabh~\cite{LMS11tw} prove that Independent Set has no $(2-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{tw}}$
algorithm, under SETH. By the classical argument~\cite{IPZ01,LMS11}, ETH implies that Independent
Set on $n$ vertices has no $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm. Both theorems cite a refuted hypothesis, yet they
end up in different places. The second has its conclusion refuted: \A, Corollary~7, gives
$O(2^{\delta n})$ for every $\delta>0$ (row B13). The first survives: its proof reduces from
CNF-SAT with $m$ clauses of unbounded width, which the new algorithms do not reach (row B1,
\cref{sec:width}). We return to this pair in \cref{sec:areas}.
\end{example}

\subsection{The naive route and the principle}
At first one might expect every SETH-based bound to fall with SETH, and every ETH-based bound with
ETH. However, \cref{ex:running} shows that this is wrong in both directions. A bound can survive
because its proof never needs bounded width, and a refutation can contradict a conclusion only
through an algorithm, never through the reduction that proved the bound. Instead, we rely on two
rules. A row falls only if the premise its proof uses falls. And a row gains an algorithm only if
some reduction runs in the other direction: from $X$ to $k$-SAT with a linear number of variables
(the SERF framework of~\cite{IPZ01}, and \A, Corollary~7), or from more general theorems inside the
manuscripts that the top-level statements do not expose (our own sparse results, \cref{sec:new}).
Most rows therefore lose a premise and gain nothing. We say so row by row.

\subsection{Contributions}
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We separate bounded from unbounded width. We prove that \cref{imp:A} counts the models of CNFs
of any width with at most $n\cdot2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses in time $2^{o(n)}$, where
$L(n)=(\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n}$, and that nothing follows at $n^{1+\Omega(1)}$ clauses
(\cref{sec:width}).
\item We classify $33$ hypotheses: $12$ are refuted, $20$ survive and $1$ is mixed
(\cref{sec:tableA}).
\item We classify $27$ conditional theorems: $3$ have their conclusion refuted, $1$ fires, $10$ lose
their premise, $9$ survive and $4$ are mixed (\cref{sec:tableB}).
\item We list the new algorithms that follow, and separate those that need only the formalised
top-level theorems from those that need internal results of \A{} (\cref{sec:new}).
\item We read the map by area and relate it to the literature
(\cref{sec:areas,sec:related}).
\end{itemize}
Much of the classification is bookkeeping that an expert in each area could do. What we add is one
map across the areas, with each row checked against its primary source where one exists.

\subsection{Sources and status of each claim}\label{sec:sources}
The map was compiled from the primary sources. For every row tagged \Lit{} in
the lists of \cref{sec:tableA,sec:tableB} we checked the wording against the abstract or full text of the cited
source (arXiv, ECCC, DROPS, journal, or authors' copy). Rows tagged \Fol{} are folklore
conjectures without a single primary statement; \Inf{} marks our own inference, and \Own{} our own
results. In an earlier pass, nine rows rested on secondary sources or on sources we had not
re-read (ETH itself; the CIP duality; Williams' Max-2-CSP algorithm; Feige's hypothesis;
P\u{a}tra\c{s}cu--Williams, twice; Cai--Juedes; Chen--Huang--Kanj--Xia; Marx's treewidth paper).
We re-verified all nine against the primary text or abstract for this note, so no row now rests on
a secondary source. Theorem numbers refer to the versions named in the bibliography.

The working map behind this note, \repo{research/fine-grained-map.md}, names for each row the source
we checked, with its URL where there is one. It is in our public repository
\url{https://github.com/xennygrimmato/eth-hamiltonicity}~\cite{TulsyanRepo26}, together with the
Lean proofs of our own results that we cite. Rows B14--B17 and B23 of this note correct the map at
the commit we cite: they add priority credits and split B16 by what is machine-checked. We separate three kinds of claim. \emph{Proved on
paper}: \cref{prop:frontier} and the observations of \cref{sec:new}. \emph{Machine-checked in
Lean}: only the results of ours that \cref{sec:new} labels so; each uses only the standard axioms
and is built on the labs' own Lean developments. \emph{Conjectured, not claimed}: the further
sparse problems of \cref{sec:new}. Everything rests on \A{} and \Oa.

\section{Bounded width falls, unbounded width stands}\label{sec:width}
% =====================================================================

In this section we state three facts used throughout, derive the density frontier of the new
algorithms, and name the hypothesis that survives.

\subsection{Three facts}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(F\arabic*)}]
\item\label{F1} $s_k=0$ for every $k$. This refutes ETH, SETH, $\#$ETH, $\#$SETH, $\oplus$SETH and
NSETH in their $k$-CNF forms.
\item\label{F2} \emph{Local witnesses.} A problem with complexity parameter $n$ whose yes-instances
have an $O(n)$-bit witness checkable by an $O(n)$-gate circuit, or by bounded-width clauses on
$O(n)$ variables, gets deterministic time $2^{\delta n}$ for every $\delta>0$ by Tseitin's
transformation. Counts follow when the encoding is parsimonious. This is the SERF
route~\cite{IPZ01}; \A, Corollary~7, uses it with a cardinality circuit of $O(n)$ gates.
\item\label{F3} \emph{The width frontier} (\cref{prop:frontier} below). CNFs of arbitrary width are
reached only when the number of clauses is barely superlinear.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The width reduction and the density frontier}
Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{CIP06} relate CNF-SAT of bounded density to $k$-SAT by
Schuler's width reduction. While some clause $C$ has more than $k$ literals, pick $k$ of them,
$C'$, and branch on ``$C'$ is satisfied'' (replace $C$ by $C'$) versus ``$C'$ is falsified'' (set
its $k$ literals to false). The branches are disjoint, so model counts add up, and the leaves are
$k$-CNFs. This counting form is \cite[Lemma~6.1]{TulsyanQ26}. With constant $k$ it gives
$\#$CNF-SAT with $m\le cn$ clauses in time $2^{\varepsilon n}$ for all $c,\varepsilon>0$, from
either engine~\cite[Corollary~6.2]{TulsyanQ26}. The uniformity of \cref{imp:A} lets $k$ grow, and
gives a little more.

\begin{proposition}[our derivation from \cref{imp:A}]\label{prop:frontier}
Put $L(n)=(\log n)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log n}$. Assume \cref{imp:A}. For every function $f$ with
$f(n)=o(L(n))$ there is a deterministic algorithm that counts the models of CNF formulas of
arbitrary width with $n$ variables and at most $n\cdot2^{f(n)}$ clauses in time $2^{o(n)}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\le Dn$ with $\log D=f(n)$, and fix $k\ge2$, to be chosen. Run the width reduction. Along a
root-to-leaf path every ``satisfied'' branch removes one long clause, so there are at most $m$ of
them, and every ``falsified'' branch fixes $k$ variables, so there are $r\le n/k$ of them. The
leaves with exactly $r$ falsified branches number at most $\binom{m+r}{r}$~\cite[proof of
Lemma~5]{CIP06}, so there are at most
$(n/k+1)\binom{m+\lfloor n/k\rfloor}{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\le(n+1)\bigl(e(Dk+1)\bigr)^{n/k}$ leaves.
Each leaf is a $k$-CNF with $n'\le n$ variables and at most $m$ clauses. If $n'\le\sqrt n$, count
by brute force in time $2^{\sqrt n}\poly(m)$. Otherwise $\log n'\ge\frac12\log n$, so
$\log\log n'/\sqrt{\log n'}\le2\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ for $n$ large, and \cref{imp:A} counts
the leaf in time $2^{s n}$ with $s=2Ck^2\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$. The total time is at most
\[
  \poly(n,m)\cdot2^{\,(n/k)\,(f(n)+\log(k+1)+2)\;+\;sn\;+\;\sqrt n}.
\]
Take $k=\lceil\sqrt{\max\{f(n),1\}\cdot L(n)}\,\rceil$. Then $k\to\infty$, $f(n)/k\le\sqrt{f(n)/L(n)}\to0$
and $(\log k)/k\to0$, while $k/L(n)\to0$ gives $s=2C(k/L(n))^2\to0$. Every term of the exponent
is $o(n)$, and $\poly(n,m)=2^{o(n)}$ since $m\le n^{1+o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what does not follow]\label{rem:frontier}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item The clause-count branch of \cref{imp:A} does worse here: it needs $km=o(n\sqrt{\log m})$,
which allows only $m\le n(\log n)^{1/2-o(1)}$.
\item Through \Oa{} only constant density is reached explicitly, since its $O_k$-constants are not
stated; a diagonal argument gives some unbounded but non-explicit density.
\item For $m=n^{1+\Omega(1)}$, or $m\ge n\cdot2^{(\log n)^{1/4+\Omega(1)}}$, nothing follows: the
leaf count forces $k\gtrsim\log(m/n)$, where neither engine is subexponential in $n$. Hence the
statement
\begin{center}
\emph{CNF-SETH}: for every $\varepsilon>0$, CNF-SAT with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses of
unbounded width is not solvable in time $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\poly(m)$
\end{center}
survives, together with its counting, parity and nondeterministic forms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\noindent CNF-SETH is exactly the premise that many famous reductions use. Lokshtanov, Marx and
Saurabh~\cite{LMS11tw} assume that ``SAT cannot be solved in $(2-\varepsilon)^n m^{O(1)}$ time''
and stress that they need no sparsification. Backurs and Indyk~\cite{BI18} reduce from CNF-SAT
with $M$ clauses to edit distance. Bringmann's ``SETH$'$''~\cite{Bri14} is ``no $O^*((2-\delta)^N)$
algorithm for CNF-SAT''. Roditty and Vassilevska Williams~\cite[Theorem~1]{RVW13} conclude
``CNF-SAT on $n$ variables is in $O^*((2-\delta)^n)$''. And the moderate-dimension Orthogonal
Vectors conjecture follows from CNF-SETH by Williams' split-and-list reduction with $d=m$. OpenAI~\cite[\S1.1.3]{OpenAI26} make the same observation for the Fr\'echet, diameter and
treewidth bounds, and note that the LCS bounds follow from OVC. What dies is everything that
genuinely goes through sparsification to $O(n)$ clauses, or through bounded width.

\section{Hypotheses: twelve refuted, twenty survive}\label{sec:tableA}
% =====================================================================

In this section we classify the hypotheses. The list below gives them. ``Gives algorithms'' is noted
in the reason column when a refutation also yields an explicit faster algorithm for the problem
named.

\paragraph{Hypotheses, assuming the claimed refutations.}
Each row gives the hypothesis and its source, its status, and the reason.
{\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item[A1] ETH: $s_k>0$ for $k\ge3$~\cite{IP99,ImpagliazzoPaturi01}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. Either engine; $3$-SAT and $k$-SAT in $2^{o(n)}$.
\item[A2] SETH: $s_\infty=1$~\cite{ImpagliazzoPaturi01}; randomized algorithms allowed in~\cite{CDLMNOPSW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. \ref{F1}; \Oa{} suffices despite non-uniformity in $k$.
\item[A3] Density-SETH: CNF of any width with $\le cn$ clauses needs $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$~\cite{CIP06}; \cite[Conj.~2]{MT19a}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. Counting width reduction~\cite[Cor.~6.2]{TulsyanQ26}.
\item[A4] CNF-SETH: CNF-SAT $\notin2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\poly(m)$, unbounded width~\cite{LMS11tw,BI18,Bri14,RVW13}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. \Cref{prop:frontier,rem:frontier}: reach ends at $n2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses.
\item[A5] Super-SETH: for every unbounded $f$, $k$-SAT $\notin2^{n(1-f(k)/k)}$~\cite{VW21}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. It implies SETH.
\item[A6] $\#$ETH~\cite[p.~21:5]{DHMTW14}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. \A, Corollary~6; \Oa. Gives $\#3$-SAT in $2^{o(n)}$.
\item[A7] $\#$SETH, $\oplus$SETH for $k$-CNF; equivalent to $k$-$\oplus$Hitting Set and $k$-$\oplus$Set Cover hardness~\cite[Thm.~1.3]{CDLMNOPSW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. \ref{F1}.
\item[A8] $\#$CNF-SETH, $\oplus$CNF-SETH ($\poly(m)$ clauses)\Inf. \emph{Status:} survives. As A4.
\item[A9] NSETH: $\forall\varepsilon\,\exists k$, $k$-TAUT $\notin\mathrm{NTIME}[2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}]$~\cite{CGIMPS16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. A deterministic algorithm is a nondeterministic one (\A, after Corollary~5).
\item[A10] NSETH for CNF with $\poly(m)$ clauses, or for $\poly$-size circuits~\cite[Conj.~3]{MT19a}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. As A4.
\item[A11] MA-SETH, AM-SETH. \emph{Status:} refuted (2016). Williams~\cite{Wil16MA}: MA proofs in $c^n$ time, $c<2$, for $\#$SAT of sublinear-depth circuits; ``strongly refute'' both\Lit. For $k$-CNF also by \ref{F1}.
\item[A12] Gap-ETH: no $2^{o(n)}$ (randomized) algorithm distinguishes satisfiable $3$-CNFs from those not $(1-\varepsilon)$-satisfiable~\cite{Dinur16,MR16}, as quoted in~\cite{CCKLMNT17}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. An exact deterministic $2^{o(n)}$ decider distinguishes the cases, as \Oa{} notes (\S1.1.1). \cite{Man26} also records Gap-ETH as false.
\item[A13] ETH for Circuit-SAT of linear size; VSP-Circuit-SAT of size $cn$~\cite[Thm.~1.1(v)]{CDLMNOPSW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. Tseitin gives $O(n)$ variables; gives algorithms.
\item[A14] Circuit-, NC- and BP-SETH~\cite{AHVWW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. Superlinear-size representations.
\item[A15] $\mathrm{FPT}\ne\mathrm{M}[1]$ (miniaturised CNF of size $k\log n$)~\cite[\S3.5]{DT11}. Chen--Grohe: $\mathrm{M}[1]=\mathrm{FPT}$ iff $3$-SAT $\in\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{o^{\mathrm{eff}}(n)})$~\cite{CG07}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. The iff needs an effective little-$o$; \A{} (rate with absolute $C$) and \Oa{} at $k=3$ supply one. Gives algorithms for M[1]-complete problems.
\item[A16] $\mathrm{FPT}\ne\mathrm{W}[1]$. \emph{Status:} survives. ETH implies it, not conversely; \cite[\S6]{CG07} list ``ETH $\Leftrightarrow$ FPT $\ne$ W[1]?'' as open\Lit.
\item[A17] Low-dimension OVC: $\forall\varepsilon\,\exists c$, OV with $d=c\log n$ $\notin n^{2-\varepsilon}$ (``LDOVC'' of~\cite{GIKW17})\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. SETH support gone (it was via sparsification~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{BK15}); \A{} notes it does not refute OV.
\item[A18] Moderate-dimension OVC: no $n^{2-\varepsilon}\poly(d)$~\cite{GIKW17}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. Implied by CNF-SETH with $d=m$~\cite[Lemma~1]{ABV15}; premise intact.
\item[A19] $k$-OV, low and moderate dimension\Inf. \emph{Status:} survives. As A17, A18.
\item[A20] Hitting Set conjecture~\cite{AVWW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. Not known to follow from SETH; implies OVC.
\item[A21] Set Cover Conjecture: $\forall\varepsilon\,\exists k$, Set Cover with sets of size $\le k$ $\notin2^{\varepsilon n}\poly(m)$, $n$ the universe~\cite[Thm.~1.2]{CDLMNOPSW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} survives. Not implied by SETH even before 2026; the natural witness has $\Theta(n\log n)$ bits, so \ref{F2} does not apply.
\item[A22] $k$-Hitting Set, $k$-Set Splitting, $k$-NAE-SAT need $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}$ for large $k$, each equivalent to SETH~\cite[Thm.~1.1]{CDLMNOPSW16}\Lit. \emph{Status:} refuted. Equivalent to SETH; directly, they are $k$-CNFs on the universe. Gives algorithms.
\item[A23] 3SUM conjecture\Fol. \emph{Status:} survives. No SAT premise; see B26.
\item[A24] APSP; combinatorial BMM; OMv\Fol. \emph{Status:} survives. Unrelated to SAT.

\subsection{The circuit lower bound fires}\label{sec:circuits}
Williams~\cite[Theorem~6.1]{Wil13} proved that if ETH is false then $\ENP$ has no circuits of
linear size. Both manuscripts draw this consequence (\A; \Oa, Corollary~C.1), and \A{}
formalises it in Lean. Modulo the refutations, $\ENP\not\subseteq\mathrm{SIZE}(O(n))$ is therefore
unconditional. OpenAI also state the quantitative form~\cite[Corollary~1.4]{OpenAI26}: some
$L\in\ENP$ has no circuits of size $c\,n\sqrt{\log n}$ for infinitely many $n$, by Williams'
argument and the local reductions of Jahanjou, Miles and Viola~\cite{JMV15}, applied to the
constant-density case of their theorem. In a companion note~\cite{TulsyanC26} we prove the same
statement, which we arrived at independently, from Anthropic's $\#3$-SAT theorem, in the
Turing-machine form of the clause-count bound of \cref{imp:A} that its Lean development provides.
We machine-check it in Lean on top of Anthropic's own circuit-lower-bound module
(\lean{enp_not_size_n_sqrt_log}; standard axioms; fresh kernel replay). The statement is OpenAI's;
what we add is the formal proof. It holds only infinitely often, and the constant is not
explicit. It gives nothing at $n\log n$ and nothing for NEXP.
Carmosino et al.~\cite{CGIMPS16} note that disproving NSETH gives circuit lower bounds; for the
$k$-CNF form refuted here this is subsumed by the above.

\section{The new algorithms are few}\label{sec:new}
% =====================================================================

The refutations give few explicit algorithms beyond $k$-SAT itself. In this section we list those
we know of. We separate what needs only the formalised top-level statements from what needs
internal results of \A. Bhattacharjee~\cite{Bhattacharjee26} claims further weighted consequences,
which we do not list.

\subsection{From the main theorems alone}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $k$-SAT, $\#k$-SAT, $\oplus k$-SAT and $k$-TAUT; $\#$CNF-SAT of any width with $O(n)$ clauses
(either engine), and with $n2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses (\A; \cref{prop:frontier}); linear-size Circuit-SAT.
\item \A, Corollary~7: for every $\delta>0$, deterministic $O(2^{\delta n})$ algorithms that count
the independent sets, cliques, vertex covers and dominating sets of each size $s$, and the proper
$k$-colourings, of a graph on $n$ vertices with any number of edges. Decision for IS, Clique, VC
and $k$-Colouring was implied before by~\cite{IPZ01}. Counting independent sets and proper
$q$-colourings is also \Oa, Corollary~1.3. Dominating Set and the counts by size are new in~\A.
\item $k$-Hitting Set, $k$-Set Splitting, $k$-NAE-SAT, $\#2$-SAT, sparse permanent, $\#$PM, Boolean
$\#$CSP, and the integer Potts and flow hyperbolas of the Tutte plane on sparse graphs (local
encodings, \ref{F2}). $\#$PM is \Oa, Corollary~1.3, for every graph; the sparse permanent, Boolean
$\#$CSP and the hyperbolas appear earlier in~\cite{Bhattacharjee26}.
\item \emph{Vertex Cover in $2^{o(k)}+\poly(n)$} (obtained earlier by
Bhattacharjee~\cite[Thm.~6.13]{Bhattacharjee26}; we arrived at it independently). The LP kernel leaves at most $2k$
vertices in polynomial time~\cite[\S2.5]{CyganEtAl15}. Corollary~7 then counts vertex covers of
size $k$ in $2^{2\delta k}$ for every $\delta$. A single algorithm in time
$2^{O(k\log\log k/\sqrt{\log k})}+\poly(n)$ follows by running the explicit $n$-bound of
\cref{imp:A} on the $O(k)$-variable $3$-CNF of the proof of Corollary~7. This contradicts the
conclusion of Cai--Juedes' ETH bound (B14), consistently with their own equivalence.
\item \emph{Sparse Max-Cut and Max-$k$-SAT, unweighted.} Bhattacharjee~\cite[Rem.~6.5]{Bhattacharjee26}
notes earlier that the circuit encoding in the proof of \Oa, Corollary~1.2, gives the exact
optimum in this way; we arrived at it independently, and add the counts by value. Take $m=O(n)$ constraints
with $O(1)$-bit weights. Encode each constraint by a forced indicator variable, add an $O(m)$-gate
adder and a comparison with a target $t$, and apply Tseitin. The result is a $3$-CNF with $O(n)$
variables whose models are the assignments of value exactly $t$. Corollary~6 counts them for each
$t$, giving the exact optimum and the number of solutions of each value in $2^{\delta n}$. Weights
up to $W$ cost $O(m\log W)$ gates, so the main theorems do not cover polynomial weights.
Bhattacharjee~\cite{Bhattacharjee26} obtains them by running the algorithm of \Oa{} itself over
truncated polynomial rings, a white-box extension.
\item $\mathrm{M}[1]$-complete problems in FPT time (A15).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{From internal results of \texorpdfstring{\A}{[A]}}
Our other results use three internal statements of \A{} (a network-to-clique reduction, an
algorithm for cliques of all levels, and the existence of its ``pieces''), called \HA{}
in~\cite{Tulsyan26,TulsyanQ26}. They are not consequences of the top-level theorems. They are,
however, theorems of \A's Lean development, and our Lean proofs are built on that development. So
the results below that we call machine-checked use only the standard axioms (\lean{propext},
\lean{Classical.choice}, \lean{Quot.sound}), have passed a fresh kernel replay, and stand or fall
with \A's development.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},resume]
\item\label{it:hc} \emph{Sparse Hamiltonicity}~\cite{Tulsyan26}: the number of Hamiltonian cycles of a graph with
$n$ vertices and $O(n)$ edges in $O(2^{\delta n})$ for every $\delta$, and in
$2^{O(\max(m,n)/\sqrt{\log n})}$ in general; hence decision, search, Hamiltonian paths, sparse TSP
with polynomial weights, directed cycles and ATSP. All are machine-checked in Lean, end to end on
top of \A's development.
\item \emph{Eulerian circuits}~\cite{TulsyanE26}, a consequence of~\ref{it:hc}. Counting Eulerian circuits
is $\#$P-complete~\cite{BW05}. For every $\delta>0$ and $\Delta$ there is a machine that counts
them on graphs of maximum degree at most $\Delta$ in time $(n+2)^C2^{\delta m}$, and one machine does so for all
graphs in $(n+2)^C2^{Cm\log_2(\Delta+2)/\sqrt{\log_2(m+2)}}$. The reduction is Kotzig's bijection
with the Hamiltonian cycles of the line graph of the double subdivision. Machine-checked; the
formal object is Kotzig's transition-system definition.
\item \emph{Exact quantum amplitudes}~\cite{TulsyanQ26}: amplitudes of Clifford+T circuits with $G$
two-qubit gates in $2^{O(G/\sqrt{\log G})}$, machine-checked. The polynomial-space variant and the
marginal and sampling corollaries have written proofs only and are not claimed.
\item \emph{Deterministic factoring.} Rudich and Rousseau~\cite{RR26} used the counting engine to
prove in Lean that one deterministic Turing machine computes the least prime factor of an $n$-bit
integer within $C_k2^{n/k}$ steps for every $k$, and conjectured the rate $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$.
Before 2026 every proven deterministic bound had a fixed positive exponent, the best being
$N^{1/5+o(1)}$~\cite{Har21}. The rate $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ was proved in Lean in a public
repository~\cite{IF26} and on paper by Shunia~\cite{Shu26}, conditionally on two unrefereed
preprints. We give an independent second Lean proof, along a different route~\cite{TulsyanF26}:
one Turing machine outputs the least prime factor of $N$ within
$\lfloor C\cdot2^{Cn/\sqrt{\log_2(n+2)}}\rfloor$ steps, by evaluating a two-dimensional
Sch\"onhage--Strassen multiplication network with Anthropic's counting theorem. Machine-checked.
\item \emph{Further sparse problems, conjectured and not claimed}~\cite{TulsyanRepo26}: Steiner
Tree, FVS, Connected VC and DS, Max-Leaf; the line $T(x,1)$ of the Tutte plane (forests counted by
size) on sparse graphs, which is the line Brand, Dell and Roth~\cite{BrandDellRoth19} show
$\#$ETH-hard; sparse Max-Cut with polynomial weights. All are conditional on \HA. For the Tutte
and connectivity problems the identities are checked numerically only; none is formalised. For
Max-Cut and Max-$k$-SAT with polynomial weights, Bhattacharjee~\cite{Bhattacharjee26} gives an
earlier, independent route. It replaces \HA{} by the claim that the steps of \Oa{} remain valid
over a commutative $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra (unrefereed, not formalised).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What gets no algorithm}
Nothing parameterised by treewidth or pathwidth gets an algorithm, except binary CSP on one class of
bounded-degree graphs, with exponent $O(\mathrm{tw}/\sqrt{\log\mathrm{tw}})$~\cite{Nguyen26} (B20).
Nor does any problem in P (edit distance, LCS, Fr\'echet, diameter, OV, 3SUM, APSP, $k$-Clique), Set
Cover by universe size, Subset Sum, dense Max-CSP, dense Hamiltonicity, or a planar problem (where
$2^{O(\sqrt n)}$ is already better).

\section{Reading the map by area}\label{sec:areas}
% =====================================================================

In this section we read the two lists area by area: exact and parameterised algorithms,
approximation, problems in P, and counting and quantum.

\subsection{Exact and parameterised algorithms}
The treewidth and pathwidth bounds (B1, B2) are the clearest case of a premise that survives
because of how it is stated; this is the first half of \cref{ex:running}. Lokshtanov, Marx and
Saurabh build, from a CNF formula with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, a graph of size polynomial in
$n+m$ and treewidth roughly $n/\log_2 c$ (the precise form varies by problem). So a
$(c-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{tw}}$ algorithm would decide CNF-SAT in time $2^{(1-\delta)n}\poly(m)$.
Nothing in the reduction requires bounded width or few clauses, and \cref{rem:frontier} shows that
the new algorithms reach only $m\le n2^{o(L(n))}$. To refute B1 one would need CNF-SAT with, say,
$n^2$ clauses of width up to $n$ in time $2^{(1-\delta)n}$. The width reduction would then need
$k\approx\log n$, where the bound of \cref{imp:A} is $2^{\Theta(n(\log n)^{3/2}\log\log n)}$, far
above $2^n$.
The other half of \cref{ex:running}, row B13, is contradicted outright. Corollary~7 of \A{} is a
reduction in the other direction: it encodes Independent Set on $n$ vertices, at any density, as a
$3$-CNF with $O(n)$ variables.

The ETH-based bounds for parameterised problems (B19--B22) lose their premise, but are not
contradicted: a $2^{\delta n}$ algorithm says nothing about $f(k)n^{o(k)}$ when $k\ll n$. The two
exceptions are structural. Chen and Grohe's equivalence (A15) is an ``if and only if'', so its
other direction now yields $\mathrm{M}[1]=\mathrm{FPT}$. And Vertex Cover parameterised by solution
size has a linear kernel, so the parameter and the number of vertices coincide after
preprocessing, and the $2^{\delta n}$ algorithm of \A{} becomes a $2^{o(k)}$ algorithm (B14), as Bhattacharjee
observed first.
Whether $\mathrm{W}[1]\ne\mathrm{FPT}$ (A16) is unaffected: Chen and Grohe list its equivalence
with ETH as open, and the refutations settle ETH without touching it.

\subsection{Approximation}
Gap-ETH is refuted by any exact $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm, so it falls with ETH; the gap does not help.
The FPT-inapproximability results of Chalermsook et al.\ (B23) therefore lose their premise. So do
the ETH-hardness results of Dinur--Manurangsi, Braverman--Ko--Weinstein and
Aaronson--Impagliazzo--Moshkovitz (B24) and the ETH proof of PIH (B25). Two kinds of result
survive. The first are the re-proofs under $\mathrm{W}[1]\ne\mathrm{FPT}$: constant-factor
$k$-Clique (Lin), $k^{1/H(k)}$-factor $k$-Clique (Karthik--Khot), $k^{o(1)}$-factor $k$-Clique
(Chen--Feng--Laekhanukit--Liu) and $F(k)$-factor $k$-DomSet (Karthik--Laekhanukit--Manurangsi).
Only the ETH-based versions, such as the $k^{o(1)}$ bound of Lin, Ren, Sun and Wang, lose their
premise. The second are results whose reductions are only mildly
exponential, so that a weaker hypothesis suffices: Manurangsi's Densest-$k$-Subgraph bound, now
stated under Almost-ETH (A33). The refutations give $3$-SAT in $2^{n/(\log n)^{c}}$ for every
constant $c<1/2$. This is $2^{n^{1-o(1)}}$ but not $2^{n^{1-\gamma}}$, so Almost-ETH stands. We
expect other quasi-polynomial-size reductions (Nash, free games, 2-CSP) to give weaker but
non-trivial bounds under Almost-ETH; we have not checked this (\Inf).

\subsection{Problems in P}
Here the map is almost entirely ``survives''. Edit distance, Fr\'echet distance and diameter are
stated from CNF-SAT with $\poly(m)$ clauses (B5, B7, B8). LCS and DTW go through Most-OV or OV
with dimension $d=m$ (B6), which CNF-SETH implies. What is lost is the derivation of the
\emph{low-dimension} OV conjecture ($d=c\log n$) from SETH, which uses sparsification to $O(n)$
clauses (A17, B10). OVC itself, 3SUM, APSP and the hyperclique hypothesis were never consequences
of ETH. The dynamic lower bounds of Abboud and Vassilevska Williams (B9) are a borderline case.
They are stated through sparsification, but by our reading proved by constructions that also run
from CNF-SETH. The barrier of Carmosino et al.\ (B26), that NSETH forbids deterministic
fine-grained reductions from SAT to 3SUM and APSP, disappears for $k$-SAT; no such reduction is
known.

\subsection{Counting and quantum}
Counting statements behave like decision ones. $\#$ETH and the $k$-CNF form of $\#$SETH fall
(A6, A7). Lower bounds proved by local reductions on sparse instances are contradicted by explicit
algorithms (B17): Corollaries~6 and~7 of \A{} count independent sets and models of $2$-CNFs at any
density, and local encodings handle sparse permanents, perfect matchings and the integer hyperbolas
of the Tutte plane. The non-local cases need \HA{}: Hamiltonian cycles are machine-checked
(B16), while forests, the line $T(x,1)$, are only conjectured (B17). Dense permanents,
and the Tutte plane on dense graphs, are untouched. For the quantum statements (B18) the same split
appears: sparse Clifford+T circuits fall under \HA, while dense or average-case conjectures
survive~\cite[\S6]{TulsyanQ26}.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

We organise related work by idea: collections of ETH-based lower bounds, the structure of SETH and
its variants, and the first responses to the refutations.

\paragraph{Collections of lower bounds.} Lokshtanov, Marx and Saurabh~\cite{LMS11} survey lower
bounds based on ETH, and so does Chapter~14 of the textbook of Cygan et al.~\cite{CyganEtAl15}. Both were written to \emph{use} the hypotheses. In contrast, we take each
bound as an object and ask which of its premises the refutations remove.

\paragraph{The structure of the hypotheses.} Closest to our work is Section~1.1.3 of OpenAI's
manuscript~\cite{OpenAI26}, which already separates fixed from unbounded width for the Fr\'echet,
diameter and treewidth bounds and points to OVC for LCS. In contrast, we classify each row, including
the approximation, parameterised and counting bounds, against the premise its proof uses. Cygan et
al.~\cite{CDLMNOPSW16} map which problems are equivalent to SETH and which are tied to the Set Cover Conjecture
instead. Calabro, Impagliazzo and Paturi~\cite{CIP06} relate clause width to clause density, and
Gao, Impagliazzo, Kolokolova and Williams~\cite{GIKW17} separate low- and moderate-dimension OV.
Carmosino et al.~\cite{CGIMPS16} introduce the nondeterministic variants. These works draw the
distinctions our map depends on. In contrast, they ask which hypotheses imply which; we ask what is
left once the $k$-CNF forms are false. The width--density relation of~\cite{CIP06} becomes the
dividing line of \cref{sec:width}.

\paragraph{Responses to the refutations.} Manurangsi~\cite{Man26} restates his Densest-$k$-Subgraph
bound under Almost-ETH. Bhattacharjee~\cite{Bhattacharjee26} extends the algorithm of \Oa{} to
weighted sums, Nguyen~\cite{Nguyen26} decides binary CSP with a sublinear treewidth exponent on one
class of graphs, and Rudich and Rousseau~\cite{RR26} factor in time $2^{o(n)}$. Our own notes count
Hamiltonian cycles~\cite{Tulsyan26}, Eulerian circuits~\cite{TulsyanE26} and quantum
amplitudes~\cite{TulsyanQ26}, factor~\cite{TulsyanF26}, and machine-check OpenAI's superlinear circuit lower
bound~\cite{TulsyanC26}; \cite[Table~2]{TulsyanQ26} classifies the fine-grained quantum-supremacy
conjectures, which row B18 summarises. These works add algorithms one problem at a time. In
contrast, this note asks what the refutations do to the hypotheses and lower bounds themselves.

Three points stand out. First, the most-cited SETH lower bounds survive with an intact premise:
those for treewidth DP and for edit distance, Fr\'echet distance, LCS and diameter. They are proved
from CNF-SAT with polynomially many clauses. What dies is ``$k$-SETH $\Rightarrow$ low-dimension
OVC'' and anything that genuinely needs sparsification. Second, Gap-ETH is refuted. So the Gap-ETH
programme in parameterised inapproximability and the ETH proof of PIH lose their premises; the parts
re-proved under W[1] $\ne$ FPT survive. Third, the frontier of the new algorithms in clause density
is sharp in an unusual way: $n\cdot2^{o(L(n))}$ clauses through the explicit rate of \A, constant
density through \Oa, and nothing at $n^{1+\Omega(1)}$.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The map stands or falls with the claimed refutations}~\cite{Anthropic26,OpenAI26}, whose
expert review is pending. If they fail, every ``refuted'' becomes ``open'' and every ``premise
refuted'' reverts to a valid conditional theorem; \cref{prop:frontier} and the observations of
\cref{sec:new} lose their proofs. Results marked \HA{} use internal statements of \A{} that are not
consequences of its top-level theorems. They are theorems of \A's Lean development, so these
results stand or fall with that development.
\item \emph{A refutation removes premises.} Except through \ref{F2} and the network theorems, it gives
no algorithm, and ``premise refuted'' is never evidence that a conclusion is false.
\item \emph{The sources were checked, but the map is not exhaustive.} Every row was checked against
its primary source; the nine rows that earlier rested on secondary sources were re-verified for
this note (\cref{sec:sources}), and no row is now secondary. Rows marked \Fol{} have no single
primary statement. Rows marked \Inf{} are our inferences and are argued only in the reason column.
B8 omits later diameter tradeoffs. \A{} reports more than $800$ papers with results proved only
under ETH or a hypothesis implying it.
\item \emph{Our written arguments are not machine-checked.} \Cref{prop:frontier} and the
observations (iv)--(v) of \cref{sec:new} are short written arguments, produced with AI assistance
and not machine-checked or reviewed by a human expert. Only the results so labelled in
\cref{sec:new} are machine-checked, on top of the labs' Lean developments. No human has yet checked
that their formal statements match the printed ones.
\item \emph{Nothing is practical.} \A{} notes that its bound does not beat $2^n$ for $3$-CNFs with
$5n$ clauses unless $n>10^{10\,000}$. All algorithms here are galactic.
\end{itemize}
